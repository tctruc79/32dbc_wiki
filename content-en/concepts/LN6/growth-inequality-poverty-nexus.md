# Growth-Inequality-Poverty Nexus

## Definition

**Growth-inequality-poverty triangle** — a framework analyzing the 3-way relationship between
economic growth, income/consumption-distribution inequality, and poverty: growth is a NECESSARY
but not SUFFICIENT condition for poverty reduction — its effectiveness depends on how growth's
gains are distributed (Bourguignon; Ravallion; Kakwani & Pernia 2000, via
[[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]]). This cluster's second core concept
is that **poverty is not a single unified concept** — the same population can be classified
differently depending on which measure is used: **monetary poverty** (consumption/income below a
threshold), **multidimensional poverty** (the Alkire-Foster method — weighted deprivations across
multiple health/education/living-standard indicators, via
[[l63-tran-alkire-klasen-2015-monetary-multidimensional-poverty-vietnam]]), and **energy poverty**
(energy costs high relative to income, or lack of electricity access, via
[[l65-nguyen-2019-energy-transition-poverty-inequality-vietnam]]) — these measures DIVERGE
substantially and cannot substitute for one another.

## Theoretical Origins

- **Foster, Greer & Thorbecke — FGT (1984)** — the standard family of poverty indices (poverty
  rate, poverty gap, squared poverty gap) used throughout the monetary-poverty literature,
  foundational for [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]].
- **Datt & Ravallion (1991)**, extended by **Kolenikov & Shorrocks (2005)** — decomposing the
  change in poverty into a growth component (holding distribution constant) and a
  redistribution/inequality component (holding total consumption constant) — L64's central
  decomposition tool.
- **Kakwani & Pernia (2000)** — the pro-poor growth index φ = 1 + D/G, classifying growth from
  "anti-poor" to "highly pro-poor" depending on the ratio of the inequality to growth component.
- **Sen (1979, 1981) — the capability approach** — the philosophical foundation for
  multidimensional poverty: improving OUTCOMES/human development matters more than changing
  INPUTS (income/consumption alone); leading to the **Alkire & Foster (2011) method** — the
  central multidimensional-poverty measurement method for L63.
- **The energy ladder model** (Hosier & Dowd 1987) vs. the **energy stacking model** (Han et al.
  2018) — 2 competing hypotheses about how households transition energy sources as income
  changes, L65's theoretical foundation.

## Which Course Sources Discuss It (3 Lenses on Measuring Vietnam Poverty)

There is no single "trunk" paper — LN6's 3 Vietnam papers form **3 complementary lenses** on the
same growth-inequality-poverty triangle, differing in which poverty measure they use:

- [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]] — the **standard monetary** lens:
  the FGT index + Datt-Ravallion decomposition + Kakwani-Pernia pro-poor index, VLSS/VHLSS
  1993–2008. This is exactly the paper behind [[k31-final-exam]] Q2.
- [[l63-tran-alkire-klasen-2015-monetary-multidimensional-poverty-vietnam]] — the
  **multidimensional/capability** lens: the Alkire-Foster method directly contrasted with
  monetary poverty on the same 2007/2008/2010 panel data, finding <50% overlap and even larger
  dynamic mismatch.
- [[l65-nguyen-2019-energy-transition-poverty-inequality-vietnam]] — the **energy** lens:
  extending poverty measurement to energy cost/access, VHLSS 2004–2016, finding energy-cost
  poverty ROSE even as other measures FELL.

## Debates/Tensions Worth Remembering for the Exam

- **Poverty measures DIVERGE — independently confirmed twice**:
  [[l63-tran-alkire-klasen-2015-monetary-multidimensional-poverty-vietnam]] finds <50% overlap
  between monetary and multidimensional poor, with monetary poverty falling clearly faster.
  [[l65-nguyen-2019-energy-transition-poverty-inequality-vietnam]] INDEPENDENTLY confirms the same
  phenomenon with a different measure pair: income/electricity poverty fell sharply but
  energy-cost poverty ROSE 2004–2016. This is the cluster's strongest connection — two different
  Vietnam papers both concluding that "poverty reduction" is not a unified phenomenon but depends
  on which measure is chosen.
- **Growth-only (1990s) vs. growth-with-equity (2000s)**:
  [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]] shows the ABSOLUTE pace of poverty
  reduction was slower in the 2000s than the 1990s, yet the pro-poor index is much higher (1.80
  vs. 0.90) because inequality FELL instead of ROSE — a paradox requiring a clear distinction
  between "the pace of poverty reduction" and "the quality/equity of growth" when answering exam
  questions.
- **A specific inequality can RISE within an overall falling trend**: L65 shows the OVERALL
  energy-expenditure Gini falls faster than income/consumption, but the coal & biomass-specific
  Gini ROSE SHARPLY (0.52→0.79) — the same logic as L64 showing overall GDP/consumption
  inequality can mask worsening inequality in specific regions/groups (e.g., North Central
  Coast, ethnic minorities).

## Connection to Vietnam

All 3 papers use Vietnam as their direct context — the densest Vietnam-focused paper cluster in
the entire wiki (3/5 of LN6's readings). The synthesized picture: Vietnam has achieved impressive
monetary poverty reduction (58%→14.5%, 1993–2008) but slower progress on non-monetary dimensions
(nutrition, health — L63) and even REGRESSION on the energy-cost dimension (L65); inequality
shifted from RISING (1990s) to FALLING (2000s) but remains heavily concentrated in rural areas,
the northern mountains, and ethnic minorities across ALL 3 papers (a notable geographic
convergence — connecting to [[livelihood-vulnerability-index]] LN4, which identifies the same
region as most climate-vulnerable). Connects to
[[l26-huynh-tran-2025-fdi-informal-economy]] (LN2): poverty/unemployment are key drivers of the
informal economy — sharing the same targeted-social-safety-net policy logic.
