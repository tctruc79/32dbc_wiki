# Technology Upgrading

**Definition**: the process of upgrading an economy's technological capability to deepen and
diversify industrial activity, thereby raising growth potential. Failure to build technological
capability → slower long-run growth.

## Three Analytical Dimensions (per the Essay Instructions)

Technology upgrading is synthesized at 3 levels: **firm – sector – macro**; each level has its
own challenges and uncertainties for Vietnam ([[essays-instructions]], Topic Area 1).

## Related Topics on the Essay List

Leapfrogging, sectoral catch-up, technology upgrading in natural resource-intensive industries,
innovation policy, industrial policy in global value chains, experimentalist governance.

## Empirical Evidence from LN6 (2 of 3 Levels — Updated 2026-08-01)

Once LN6 ("Innovation, Technology, Growth, Inequality and Poverty") was ingested, its first 2
papers provide CONCRETE empirical evidence for 2 of the 3 levels this concept posits
(firm–sector–macro) — though NEITHER paper directly measures "technological capability" in the
exact terminology of the essay instructions, so this page keeps `status: stub`, only adding
relevant empirical grounding.

- **Macro/country level**: [[l61-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-tfp-technology-shifters]] directly
  measures "technology shifters" (4 indices: infrastructure, finance, technology, human capital)
  affecting TFP growth across 190 countries — providing a concrete QUANTITATIVE framework for
  "macro-level technology upgrading" instead of purely qualitative description as the essay
  instructions suggest. A key finding: TC and TFP growth are NEGATIVE across ALL income groups —
  empirical evidence that technology-upgrading FAILURE is more widespread than expected, even in
  high-income countries.
- **Firm level**: [[l62-loof-heshmati-2006-innovation-performance]] directly measures the
  innovation input → innovation output → performance causal chain at the firm level (Sweden),
  using a CDM-style knowledge production function — providing a methodological template for
  "firm-level technology upgrading".
- **Sector level — still missing**: no reading in the wiki yet measures technology upgrading at
  the SECTOR level (between firm and macro) — this gap remains open after LN6.
