# Trade War and Protectionism

## Definition

**Trade war** — an escalating spiral of retaliatory tariffs/trade barriers between major
economies, initiated between the US and China in 2018 and continuing across multiple US
presidencies. LN10 examines this phenomenon across 3 LAYERS of analysis: the
**theoretical/global simulation layer** (CGE, policy expectations —
[[l101-robinson-thierfelder-2024-us-trade-policy-cge]],
[[l102-alessandria-2025-trade-war-tariff-risk]]), the **behavioral/intermediate-effect layer**
(tariff evasion, trade diversion — [[l103-che-2025-tariff-evasion-trade-war]],
[[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]],
[[l105-almazan-gomez-2025-regional-exposure-trade-wars-eu]]), and the **direct Vietnam-evidence
layer** ([[l106-dang-2024-vietnam-exports-us-trade-war-did]],
[[l107-toai-2025-vietnam-import-export-trade-war]]). The concept running through all 7 papers:
**trade diversion** — when a tariff blocks one trade flow, that flow does NOT disappear but
redirects to another partner/channel, with unevenly distributed consequences across
countries/regions/sectors.

## Theoretical origins

- **Computable General Equilibrium (CGE)** — the standard multi-sector/multi-country simulation
  model for assessing hypothetical ("what if") trade-policy impacts, the foundation of
  [[l101-robinson-thierfelder-2024-us-trade-policy-cge]].
- **Dynamic trade-policy expectations models** — dynamic models of export behavior under
  time-varying policy uncertainty, differing from static CGE by DIRECTLY modeling changing
  expectations, the foundation of [[l102-alessandria-2025-trade-war-tariff-risk]].
- **Horizontal vs. Vertical Trade Diversion (HTD/VTD)** — the classification framework of
  [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]]: HTD = diverting goods to countries
  SIMILAR to the original market (comparable income/preferences); VTD = diverting goods DOWN THE
  QUALITY LADDER to lower-income countries — a key distinction for understanding how China
  responded to US tariffs.
- **Partial equilibrium + interregional input-output** — the combined framework in
  [[l105-almazan-gomez-2025-regional-exposure-trade-wars-eu]] to measure regional SPILLOVER
  impacts (direct + indirect via intra-bloc supply chains).
- **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)** — the standard causal-inference tool for measuring the
  trade war's impact on a specific group (China's tariffed goods in L103, Vietnam in L106)
  relative to an unaffected control group.

## Debates/tensions worth remembering for the exam

- **Trade-war "wins" may be illusory — 2 independent papers both warn of this**:
  [[l101-robinson-thierfelder-2024-us-trade-policy-cge]] (theoretical simulation) and
  [[l103-che-2025-tariff-evasion-trade-war]] (empirical evidence) both conclude US protectionist
  policy generates large costs/limited benefits — L103 shows CONCRETELY that most (69.70%) of
  the "reduced deficit" figure the Trump administration touted is illusory, due to tariff
  evasion rather than a genuine trade-balance improvement.
- **Trade diversion does NOT follow the "intuitive" direction (HTD) but a quality ladder
  (VTD)**: [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]] rebuts the conventional
  expectation that China would divert goods to US-similar countries — instead diverting DOWN to
  the Global South while UPGRADING product quality as it does so — a memorable counterintuitive
  finding for comparison-style questions.
- **Vietnam is both a BENEFICIARY and a RISK POINT within the same trade-diversion picture**:
  [[l106-dang-2024-vietnam-exports-us-trade-war-did]]/[[l107-toai-2025-vietnam-import-export-trade-war]]
  show Vietnam clearly benefiting (exports +14% to +40%) — but read alongside
  [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]] (China evading tariffs via VTD) and L107
  (Vietnam's dependence on Chinese inputs, low value-added), the picture is more complex: part
  of Vietnam's "export win" may be intertwined with a transshipment/assembly role for
  Chinese-origin goods — exactly the transshipment risk L107 warns about.
- **Policy uncertainty changes in NATURE over time, not just in DEGREE**:
  [[l102-alessandria-2025-trade-war-tariff-risk]] shows the probability of the trade war ending
  fell from >70% (2018) to 21% (2023) — not simply "higher tariffs" but expectations about the
  PERMANENCE of protectionist policy fundamentally shifted across multiple presidencies.

## Relevance to Vietnam

This is the course's FINAL session, and Vietnam is the direct context for 2/7 papers (L106,
L107) — serving as a fitting course-wide synthesis: Vietnam is a small, open, deeply integrated
economy, STRONGLY affected by external shocks/policies (a theme running through all 10 lectures
— connecting to [[institutions]] LN2, [[l26-huynh-tran-2025-fdi-informal-economy]] LN2 on
FDI/governance, and LN3 on financial crises). The trade-war picture shows Vietnam genuinely
benefiting (sharply higher exports) but with accompanying long-term structural risks (dependence
on Chinese inputs, import/export imbalance, risk of anti-circumvention investigation) — a policy
lesson on diversifying trade partners and raising domestic value-added rather than relying
solely on the trade-diversion windfall.
