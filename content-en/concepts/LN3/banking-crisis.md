# Banking Crisis

## Definition

**Banking crisis**: a period during which the banking sector's capacity to intermediate funds
is severely impaired, because equity holders are the first to bear losses
([[l31-baron-2021-banking-crises-without-panics]]).

**Panic**: a narrower subset of banking crisis — a period of sudden, severe withdrawal of funds
by bank creditors from a large part of the banking system. The key distinction: **a banking
crisis does not necessarily involve a panic** — Baron, Verner & Xiong (2021) show that large
declines in bank equity still produce serious macroeconomic consequences even without a panic
(a "quiet crisis").

## Theoretical origins

- **The panic-centric school**: Diamond & Dybvig (1983) — funding long-term assets with
  short-term debt makes banks vulnerable to self-fulfilling panics even while still solvent.
  Bernanke (2018) attributes much of the severity of the Great Recession to panic in the
  funding/securitization markets following the collapse of Lehman Brothers.
- **The bank-capital-centric school** (Calomiris & Mason 2003; Greenlaw et al. 2008; Admati &
  Hellwig 2014): emphasizes that a **bank capital crunch** caused by asset losses — not
  panic — is the key policy variable to monitor.
- [[l31-baron-2021-banking-crises-without-panics]] reconciles the two schools: panic is an
  **amplifying** mechanism for the consequences, not a necessary condition — on average, panics
  occur only after bank equity has already lost 55% of its total decline.

## Which course sources discuss it

- [[l31-baron-2021-banking-crises-without-panics]] — the central source for this concept, with
  data from 46 countries, 1870–2016.
- [[l32-jiang-2019-competition-bank-liquidity-creation]] — connects via the **liquidity

  create liquidity, a mechanism that may make banks more vulnerable ahead of a crisis.

  wake of the GFC and the pandemic, with particular attention to the role of emerging-market
  banks.
- [[l36-minford-2025-monetary-policy-lessons]] — a long-run macro monetary-policy perspective
  (1970s–present), with lessons on post-crisis stabilization.

## Connection to Vietnam

Vietnam is an emerging banking market (per L35, assessed as "particularly vulnerable" to
geopolitical financial fragmentation). L26 (Huynh & Tran 2025, LN2) already showed that local
governance quality affects Vietnam's informal economy — connecting to the question of
provincial-level bank supervision/regulation. A possible essay topic: comparing Baron et al.'s
"bank equity vs. panic" framework against Vietnamese banking data (if available), or analyzing
the impact of foreign bank competition/entry on liquidity creation using the L32 model.
