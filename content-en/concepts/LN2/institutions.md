# Institutions

**Definition (North, 1990, quoted in Alesina & Giuliano 2015)**: "the humanly devised
constraints that structure human interactions" — comprising formal constraints (rules, laws,
constitutions) and informal constraints (norms, conventions, self-imposed codes of conduct).
Formal rules are created by the polity; informal norms are part of **culture**. Central to
[[ln2-governance-institutions-policy-making]].

## Theoretical strands in LN2

1. **Historical origins** — [[l21-acemoglu-2001-colonial-origins]]: present-day institutions
   are rooted in colonial strategy (extractive vs. Neo-European), determined by settler
   mortality, and persist over time. This is also the theoretical foundation for the
   **reversal of fortune** discussed in [[deep-roots-of-development]] (a tension: AJR argue
   that institutions are determined by settler mortality, but Spolaore & Wacziarg (2013) show
   the reversal disappears once Europe is added / ancestry is adjusted for — institutions do
   not explain all of the persistence).
2. **Corruption as institutional failure** — [[l22-mauro-1995-corruption-growth]]: low
   bureaucratic efficiency / high corruption → lower investment, lower growth.
3. **Property rights** — [[l23-besley-ghatak-2010-property-rights]]: institutions that protect
   property rights are a precondition for efficient resource allocation; there is no
   one-size-fits-all formula across contexts.
4. **Culture ↔ institutions** — [[l24-alesina-giuliano-2015-culture-institutions]]: a
   bidirectional, co-endogenous relationship — institutions are not the purely exogenous
   variable that the AJR framework implies.
5. **Application to Vietnam** — [[l26-huynh-tran-2025-fdi-informal-economy]]: local governance
   quality (a provincial-level proxy for institutions) is a channel through which FDI reduces
   the informal economy.

## Debates/tensions worth remembering for the exam

- **Institutions as an exogenous variable (AJR) vs. co-endogenous with culture (Alesina &
  Giuliano)**: AJR use settler mortality as an instrument, implying institutions can be
  causally isolated; Alesina & Giuliano push back — culture and institutions co-evolve, making
  one-directional causal separation difficult.
- **Institutions as a "black box"**: the LN2 slides themselves acknowledge that AJR do not
  explain the specific mechanism (property rights, expropriation risk) — Besley & Ghatak open
  this black box in more detail.
- Connection to [[deep-roots-of-development]]: institutions are only ONE channel within the
  deep-roots literature (alongside geography and ancestry) — persistence should not be
  attributed to institutions alone.

## Connection to Vietnam

[[l26-huynh-tran-2025-fdi-informal-economy]] provides direct evidence: provincial-level local
governance quality (2006–2021) is a channel through which FDI reduces the informal economy,
alongside the growth channel. Essay suggestion: compare inter-provincial governance quality in
Vietnam as an empirical application of institutions economics.
