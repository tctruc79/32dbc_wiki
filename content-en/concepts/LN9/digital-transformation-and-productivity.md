# Digital Transformation and Productivity

## Definition

**Digitalization** — the process by which digital technology (ICT, software, data) permeates
economic activity at MULTIPLE levels: **national** (the digital economy's share of GDP —
[[l94-vietduc-2024-digital-economy-vietnam]]), **policy/strategy** (national AI
investment/regulatory framework — [[l93-pham-2024-ai-development-vietnam-review]]), **sectoral**
(barriers to technology adoption in construction —
[[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]]), and **firm** (digitalization
intensity and financial performance —
[[l96-kuosmanen-2026-digital-adoption-firm-performance-finland]]). The common thread across the 4
papers: digitalization is NOT a binary event (yes/no) but a PROCESS with a measurable intensity
(digital spillover as %GDP, firm-level Digital Intensity Index), and its relationship with
productivity/performance is NOT linear/automatic — it depends on behavioral barriers, firm size,
and can weaken over time.

## Theoretical origins

- **International digital-economy measurement frameworks** — 4 competing standards
  [[l94-vietduc-2024-digital-economy-vietnam]] compares before choosing its own direction: ITU
  IDI (Access/Use/Skills), WEF NRI (4 readiness groups), OECD DESI (5 dimensions), G20 DETF (35
  indicators, 4 groups) — the paper chooses the national account statistics (NAS) method over
  these composite indices because it allows direct calculation of GDP contribution.
- **The Eurostat Digital Intensity Index (DII)** — a multi-dimensional index measuring digital
  maturity at the FIRM level (not national level), the measurement foundation for
  [[l96-kuosmanen-2026-digital-adoption-firm-performance-finland]].
- **The technology-adoption-barriers literature** — the EFA (exploratory factor analysis)
  framework classifying adoption barriers into factor groups, used in
  [[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]], sharing a methodological family
  with other technology-barrier studies in the wiki (e.g.
  [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] LN8 uses similar surveys/interviews for
  circular-economy barriers).

## Debates/tensions worth remembering for the exam

- **Digitalization barriers are CULTURAL/BEHAVIORAL, not technological** — the finding in
  [[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]] (social/habitual resistance
  ranks top of 31 barriers) EXACTLY repeats the pattern seen in
  [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] (LN8, cultural barriers rank top,
  technology ranks LAST 15/15) — 2 entirely different fields (Vietnamese construction
  digitalization vs. EU circular economy) converge on one argument: the biggest transition
  barrier in many contexts is PEOPLE/HABITS, not technical capability.
- **Digitalization benefits can weaken over time** —
  [[l96-kuosmanen-2026-digital-adoption-firm-performance-finland]] shows a strong
  DII–productivity correlation in early years that WEAKENS (partly due to COVID-19, partly
  because lower-productivity firms also began digitalizing) — caution is needed before treating
  "digitalization always raises productivity" as a fixed law.
- **National policy lags behind sectoral/firm practice**:
  [[l93-pham-2024-ai-development-vietnam-review]] shows Vietnam lags on AI POLICY, while
  [[l94-vietduc-2024-digital-economy-vietnam]] shows the digital economy STILL grows rapidly
  (7.08% of GDP by 2019) — suggesting current digitalization growth is largely market/firm-driven
  rather than led by state policy.

## Relevance to Vietnam

3/4 papers (L93, L94, L95) use Vietnam as their direct context — the synthesized picture:
Vietnam's digital economy is growing rapidly in SCALE (7.08% of GDP by 2019, targeting 20–30% of
GDP by 2025–2030) but remains weak on supporting POLICY (L93) and faces CULTURAL/BEHAVIORAL
barriers at the specific sectoral level (L95, construction). The Finland paper (L96) serves as a
"benchmark" from a developed country — showing that even in Finland, digitalization's benefits
are uneven/not absolutely sustained, suggesting Vietnam should not expect digitalization to be a
"silver bullet" that automatically solves productivity problems.
