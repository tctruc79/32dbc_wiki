# Middle-Income Trap

**Definition**: a situation in which an economy grows rapidly thanks to low-cost advantages and
technological catch-up, reaches middle-income status, and then **stalls for a prolonged
period** — losing its low-cost advantage to poorer countries while not yet having built enough
innovative capacity to compete with high-income countries.

**Origin of the concept**: Gill & Kharas (2015) — an economy squeezed between low-wage
competition from poorer countries (in mature industries) and the innovative capacity of richer
countries (in industries undergoing technological change) ([[ln1-economic-development]]).

## ⚡ Debate: empirical evidence that CONTRADICTS the trap

[[l11-patel-2021-unconditional-convergence]] (LN1 slides, part 8/14): testing via the γ
coefficient shows that **after 1985 middle-income countries grew 0.50–0.75 percentage points
FASTER** than other groups — an inverted U-shape, with middle-income countries growing fastest
rather than being "stuck" at all. The trap is a popular narrative in policy circles, but
cross-country data does not support it.

→ A tension worth remembering for the exam/essay: **the professor's essay topics use the
middle-income trap as their framing** ([[essays-instructions]]), while the required reading L11
empirically refutes it. A possible reconciliation: the trap may still hold at the mechanism
level (technology-upgrading failure within individual countries) even though it does not show
up in cross-country averages.

**Additional mechanism-level evidence from LN6 (2026-08-01)**:
[[l61-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-tfp-technology-shifters]] (LN6) provides concrete evidence
supporting the "mechanism-level" reconciliation above — the paper finds technical change (TC)
and TFP growth are NEGATIVE across **ALL** country income groups (including both the very-low
and very-high groups) in the technology index model, 1996–2013, even though input elasticities
(labor/capital/energy) remain positive and returns to scale > 1. In other words: observed GDP
growth may stem from input ACCUMULATION rather than genuine productivity improvement —
consistent with the "technology-upgrading failure" mechanism the middle-income trap posits,
even though it does not clearly show up in average GDP/capita growth (per L11).
