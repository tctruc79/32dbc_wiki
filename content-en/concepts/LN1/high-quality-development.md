# High-Quality Economic Development (HQED)

**Definition**: a Chinese policy concept (from the 19th CPC National Congress) about shifting
the growth model from **high-speed** growth to **high-quality** growth — measured by a
multidimensional composite index built around the "new development concept" (5th Plenary
Session of the 18th Central Committee) rather than by GDP alone.

## 5 principles & measurement (per [[l15-yin-2025-china-hqed]])

5 primary indicators — **innovation, coordination, greenness, openness, sharing** — are broken
down into 11 secondary indicators and **21 concrete tertiary indicators** (R&D input/output,
the urban-rural income gap, energy consumption per unit of GDP, green coverage ratio, the
import-export-to-GDP ratio, per-capita education spending...). Weights are computed with the
**entropy method** (objective weighting based on each indicator's information entropy, avoiding
the subjective bias of methods like AHP) to build a composite score of 0–1. Spatio-temporal
analysis uses Moran's Index (spatial autocorrelation), K-means clustering, and Gaussian kernel
density estimation.

## Empirical results (China, 30 provinces, 2011–2021)

- The eastern region (Guangdong, Jiangsu, Beijing, Zhejiang, Shanghai, Shandong) clearly
  outperforms the central, northeastern, and western regions — development is "uneven,
  uncoordinated, insufficient."
- The overall trend is upward (the kernel density peak keeps shifting rightward), but at the
  same time there is **three-peak polarization** — the gap between leading and lagging
  provinces keeps widening even as the overall level improves. Spatial clustering is
  statistically significant throughout 2011–2021 (Moran's I, p<0.05).

## Connection to Vietnam

The provincial-level composite measurement framework (entropy + 5 principles + clustering +
kernel density) is directly replicable for Vietnam — tying into the professor's suggestion on
inter-provincial convergence ([[unconditional-convergence]], LN1 slide 37). The three-peak
polarization phenomenon despite an improving overall trend is an important caveat:
national-level convergence (Patel et al.) does not automatically guarantee inter-provincial
σ-convergence within a single country.
