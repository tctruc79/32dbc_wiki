# Deep Roots of Development

**Definition**: a strand of literature that looks for the **fundamental** causes (geography,
history, culture, ancestry) of development differences, rather than proximate determinants
(capital, labor, technology). Framing paper: [[l13-spolaore-2013-deep-roots]].

## Blocks of evidence (per LN1 slides)

1. **Geography**: explains 44% of the variation in income (latitude is the strongest single
   factor), but mostly through an **indirect** channel (Neolithic transmission — 71% of the
   variation in the adoption of agriculture).
2. **Institutions & reversal of fortune** (AJR 2001/2002, Nobel 2025): settler mortality →
   good/bad institutions; but the reversal disappears once Europe is added or ancestry is
   adjusted for.
3. **Populations, not locations**: persistence is a trait that people carry with them when
   they migrate (Putterman & Weil 2010, migration matrix; Easterly & Levine 2012 — European
   ancestry share).
4. **Genetic distance (FST)**: genealogical distance between populations acts as a barrier to
   the diffusion of technology/institutions (Spolaore & Wacziarg 2009).
5. **Transmission mechanism**: 4 dimensions (genetic, epigenetic, behavioural, symbolic) →
   biological / cultural / dual transmission (Jablonka & Lamb 2005).

## Key takeaway

History is persistent but **not deterministic**; understanding deep roots as **barriers to
diffusion** means policy still has a role in lowering those barriers (Japan → East Asia; the
Hong Kong demonstration effect, Romer 2009). Ignoring long-run variables leads to mis-evaluating
the effectiveness of policy.
