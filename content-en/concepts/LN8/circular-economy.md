# Circular Economy

## Definition

**Circular Economy (CE)**: first raised by 2 British environmental economists, **Pearce & Turner
(1990)**, in *Economics of Natural Resources and the Environment* — challenging the traditional
linear economy (no built-in recycling tendency, treating the environment as a "waste reservoir")
via the 1st law of thermodynamics (total energy/matter is constant in a closed system) to argue
the open-ended system should/can convert into a circular one. **The core problem**: Kirchherr et
al. (2017) analyze **114 different existing CE definitions** in the literature — CE is a
"contested" concept, with no unified definition, used via
[[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]],
[[l84-garcia-barragan-2019-defining-measuring-circular-economy]], and
[[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]] as a common starting point. The most
commonly used composite definition in the course (via Kirchherr et al. 2017): "an economic
system based on business models which replace the 'end-of-life' concept with reducing, reusing,
recycling materials in production/distribution/consumption, aiming to accomplish sustainable
development (environmental quality + economic prosperity + social equity)." CE rests on the
**3R** principles (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) extended into 10 R-strategies (R0 Refuse through R9
Recover).

## Theoretical origins

- **Pearce & Turner (1990)** — the original theoretical foundation, a linear-economy model
  (R=resource, P=production, C=consumer goods, W=waste, r=recycling) contrasted with a circular
  economy.
- **Boulding (1966), "The Economics of the Coming Spaceship Earth"** — the earth as a closed
  economic system, the economy and environment related circularly rather than linearly.
- **Leontief (1928/1991), "The Economy as a Circular Flow"** — critiques mainstream economics
  for focusing only on price theory, neglecting the material perspective (commented on by
  Samuelson 1991).
- **Ellen MacArthur Foundation (2013, 2015)** — 3 practical CE principles (design out
  waste/pollution; keep products/materials at highest use; regenerate natural ecosystems) + the
  "butterfly diagram" distinguishing technical (technosphere) and biological (biosphere) loops —
  the framework used by [[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]].
- **Kirchherr, Reike & Hekkert (2017)** — analyzing 114 existing CE definitions, producing the
  composite definition most widely used in LN8 (by Kirchherr himself in
  [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] and by
  [[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]]).
- **Hockerts & Wüstenhagen (2010)** — "emerging Davids" (new sustainable ventures) vs. "greening
  Goliaths" (large firms gradually greening) — the entrepreneurship-CE framework used by
  [[l82-heshmati-2017-review-circular-economy-implementation]].

## Which course sources discuss it (trunk + channels)

There is no single "trunk/programmatic" paper like [[entrepreneurship-and-development]] (LN5) —
instead LN8 has **2 same-author empirical case studies** (China/Sweden) as pillars, surrounded by
3 papers on **definition/measurement** and 2 **emerging channels** (employment, innovation-SDG):

- **Empirical case — China**: [[l81-su-2013-circular-economy-china]] (Su, Heshmati, Geng & Yu
  2013) — comparing CE across 4 pilot cities (Dalian/Beijing/Shanghai/Tianjin), a top-down
  governance model with 2 central agencies (MEP/NDRC).
- **Empirical case — Sweden**:
  [[l86-heshmati-rashidghalam-2021-urban-circular-economy-sweden]] (Heshmati & Rashidghalam
  2021) — a PCA composite CE index for 273 municipalities, a decentralized/market-based
  governance model (contrasting China).
- **Global review, bridging the 2 cases above**:
  [[l82-heshmati-2017-review-circular-economy-implementation]] (Heshmati 2017) — expands the
  review beyond China, introducing Sweden + an entrepreneurship lens.
- **Definition/measurement channel** (see Debates section):
  [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] (barriers, EU, large-N),
  [[l84-garcia-barragan-2019-defining-measuring-circular-economy]] (a mathematical definition),
  [[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]] (a taxonomy of 55 indicator sets).
- **Employment channel**: [[l87-nademi-kalmarzi-2025-circular-economy-unemployment]] (Nademi &
  Sedaghat Kalmarzi 2025) — panel GMM, all 8 CE indexes reduce unemployment in Europe, a
  "circular justice" framework.
- **Innovation/SDG channel (meta-view)**: [[l88-vera-2025-innovation-circular-economy-sdgs]]
  (Luengo Vera et al. 2025) — a bibliometric analysis of 1630 articles, innovation as a
  "systemic enabler" of CE-SDG, China/UK/India as the 3 scientific poles.

## Debates/tensions worth remembering for the exam

- **3 different responses to the "114 CE definitions" (Kirchherr et al. 2017)** — LN8's central
  debate: (1) [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] **ACCEPTS** the composite
  definition and proceeds to empirically measure barriers; (2)
  [[l84-garcia-barragan-2019-defining-measuring-circular-economy]] tries to **ELIMINATE**
  ambiguity via a single mathematical definition (Cₜ* = Rₜ* − Lₜ*, the economy is circular if
  Cₜ*>0); (3) [[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]] chooses **ORGANIZED
  PLURALISM** — not trying to redefine CE but building a 10-category taxonomy to help select the
  right one among 55 different measurement indicators. Three opposing directions
  (accepting/eliminating/organizing the ambiguity) from the same root problem — LN8's strongest
  comparison question pairing.
- **Technological or cultural barriers? China vs. the EU**:
  [[l81-su-2013-circular-economy-china]] and
  [[l82-heshmati-2017-review-circular-economy-implementation]] (China context) both list
  "lacking advanced technology" among top CE barriers; but
  [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] (EU context, 208-respondent survey + 47
  interviews) finds technological barriers ranking **LAST (15/15)**, with cultural barriers
  (lacking consumer interest, hesitant company culture) topping the list instead. Suggests:
  differing levels of economic development (developing vs. developed) may determine which
  barrier type is the true binding constraint.
- **CE governance: state-led top-down (China) vs. decentralized market-based (Sweden)**:
  [[l81-su-2013-circular-economy-china]] describes a 2-central-agency model (MEP/NDRC) + city
  pilot programs; [[l86-heshmati-rashidghalam-2021-urban-circular-economy-sweden]] describes an
  autonomous-municipality model + price levers (the waste-collection charge WCOL is the
  STRONGEST regression coefficient). A counterintuitive finding: in China, higher provincial
  scale/development level usually comes with better infrastructure
  ([[l71-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-urban-infrastructure-china]], LN7); in Sweden, the
  LARGEST/highest-density city (Stockholm) has the LOWEST CEI — the city-scale↔environmental/
  infrastructure-quality relationship reverses depending on institutional context.
- **CE and employment — clear macro vs. mixed micro**:
  [[l87-nademi-kalmarzi-2025-circular-economy-unemployment]] (panel GMM, Europe 2010–2019) finds
  CE UNAMBIGUOUSLY reduces unemployment across all 8 indexes; but
  [[l56-sharma-subba-2025-green-startups-sustainability]] (LN5, a green-startup review) finds a
  much more complex employment picture — green startups have a HIGHER failure rate than
  conventional businesses, struggling to formalize "green jobs" in developing countries.
  Possibly due to different levels of analysis (macro whole-CE-sector in developed Europe vs.
  individual startups globally).

## Relevance to Vietnam

Vietnam has issued a National Circular Economy Strategy to 2030 — both contrasting governance
models in LN8 (top-down China vs. decentralized/price-based Sweden) are useful references since
Vietnam's institutions are more centralized than Sweden's but more decentralized than China's.
Specific lessons: (i) avoid 2 parallel evaluation indicator systems causing overlap (the lesson
from China's MEP/NDRC, [[l81-su-2013-circular-economy-china]]); (ii) the price/waste-fee lever
is most effective in the Swedish model
([[l86-heshmati-rashidghalam-2021-urban-circular-economy-sweden]]); (iii) avoid a
"recycling-only" bias when designing a national CE indicator set
([[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]] — only 45% of global indicators cover
all 3 CE loops); (iv) consider a "circular justice" framework to avoid the CE transition
worsening inequality for informal labor in traditional recycling
([[l87-nademi-kalmarzi-2025-circular-economy-unemployment]], connecting to
[[l26-huynh-tran-2025-fdi-informal-economy]] LN2). Vietnam does NOT appear among the 3 main
scientific poles on CE-innovation-SDG (China/UK/India,
[[l88-vera-2025-innovation-circular-economy-sdgs]]) — a research gap, a plausible essay
direction.
