# K32 Mock Exam — Set 10 (LN10 compulsory + LN1 elective)

Set 10: compulsory questions 1–2 use L104 + L107 (LN10, method + main-findings angle); elective questions 3–6 use L14 + L15 (LN1, two angles each: mechanism/robustness + policy implications). ## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Sheng, Song and Zheng (Journal of International Money and Finance, 2025) examined how Chinese exporters responded to the US-China trade war. Discuss their findings concerning vertical versus horizontal trade diversion and China's choice of destination markets.

**Question 2**: Toai (Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 2025) examined the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's imports and exports. Discuss their findings concerning Vietnam's trade gains and their composition during the US-China trade war.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Sasges and Takahashi (International Economics, 2025) examined the relative influence of globalization, electricity infrastructure and privatization on Vietnam's economic development. Explain why privatization's role strengthens over time while globalization and electricity's role weakens, and why Vietnam is less policy-sensitive than China in the later period.

**Question 4**: Sasges and Takahashi (International Economics, 2025) examined the relative influence of globalization, electricity infrastructure and privatization on Vietnam's economic development. Explain the policy lesson the authors draw and one limitation of relying on this VAR framework for policy conclusions.

**Question 5**: Yin, Bai and Sun (Sustainable Futures, 2025) examined the spatiotemporal evolution of China's high-quality economic development. Explain what drives the leading provinces' advantage and why national-level improvement can coexist with rising regional polarization.

**Question 6**: Yin, Bai and Sun (Sustainable Futures, 2025) examined the spatiotemporal evolution of China's high-quality economic development. Explain the policy lesson for lagging provinces and one limitation of using a composite index to guide such policy.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — How did Chinese Exporters Manage the Trade War? (Sheng, Song and Zheng 2025)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]].

- Chinese export data by month-product around the tariff-hike period; three steps — overall US tariff effect, trade-diversion regression on destination characteristics, product quality from demand-regression residuals.
- Rejects Horizontal Trade Diversion (to countries similar to the US); confirms Vertical Trade Diversion (VTD) — high-quality goods moved DOWN to the Global South.
- Robustness: excluding countries suspected of transshipment (including Vietnam) from the sample → the VTD result HOLDS UNCHANGED.

### Question 2 — Impact of the US-China Trade War on Vietnam's Imports and Exports (Toai 2025)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l107-toai-2025-vietnam-import-export-trade-war]].

- Descriptive-qualitative, NO regression model: descriptive statistics, an import-source concentration index, regional trade-policy content analysis.
- Vietnamese exports to the US rose sharply (<span class="stat">USD 83.0bn→119.5bn</span>, 2020→2024), the US taking <span class="stat">29.5%</span> of total exports — the largest market.
- Imports from China rose ALMOST IN PARALLEL, mostly intermediate goods; the Vietnam-US surplus ballooned considerably.

### Question 3 — Assessing the Influence of Three Policies on Vietnam's Economic Development (Sasges and Takahashi 2025)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l14-sasges-2025-vietnam-policies]].

- Early period: electricity + integration relieve binding constraints (power shortages, trade closure) — large marginal effect.
- Later period: infrastructure/integration saturate, markets and institutions mature — privatization's incentive/allocation effects become the main lever.
- Vietnam is less sensitive than China in the same later period — suggesting Vietnamese institutions/markets can absorb policy shocks more smoothly.

### Question 4 — Assessing the Influence of Three Policies on Vietnam's Economic Development (Sasges and Takahashi 2025)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l14-sasges-2025-vietnam-policies]].

- Policy: continue upgrading energy infrastructure + international integration while improving the governance/competitiveness of privatized firms.
- Limitation: VAR captures dynamic correlation over time, not a clean isolation of each policy's causal weight from simultaneous global shocks (e.g., China's rise).
- Fast liberalization can bring initial volatility — sequencing and supporting institutions matter, not liberalization alone.

### Question 5 — Measurement and Spatiotemporal Dynamic Evolution of China's High-Quality Economic Development (Yin, Bai and Sun 2025)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l15-yin-2025-china-hqed]].

- Leading provinces: policy/institutional advantages, high-tech economic structure, large FDI, quality human capital — these advantages SELF-REINFORCE over time.
- Overall improvement happens because most provinces advance in absolute terms; but the pace differs sharply → the RELATIVE gap still widens.
- This is evidence that reform-and-opening benefits have NOT spread evenly — lagging provinces fall behind relatively, not necessarily in absolute terms.

### Question 6 — Measurement and Spatiotemporal Dynamic Evolution of China's High-Quality Economic Development (Yin, Bai and Sun 2025)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l15-yin-2025-china-hqed]].

- Policy: targeted innovation investment in lagging provinces, strengthen urban-rural coordination, deepen reform-and-opening and regional cooperation.
- Limitation: a composite index depends on the chosen indicators/data — it cannot fully capture newer forces like AI and the digital economy.
- Province-SPECIFIC policy is needed rather than one formula, since the sources of lag differ across regions.
