## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen (World Development, 2019) examined the impact of the GET Ahead training programme on female microfinance borrowers' empowerment. Discuss their findings concerning the impacts of the GET Ahead training programme on female microfinance borrowers' empowerment.

**Question 2**: Stein, Evers and O'Gorman (Technovation, 2026) examined the role of institutional entrepreneurs in Denmark's FinTech ecosystem development. Discuss their findings concerning the conditions institutional entrepreneurs create for Denmark's FinTech ecosystem to develop.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Productivity Analysis, 2020) examined technical change and TFP growth based on observable technology shifters. Explain why technology alone is insufficient to raise TFP, and one reason the negative TFP result should be treated cautiously.

**Question 4**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Productivity Analysis, 2020) examined technical change and TFP growth based on observable technology shifters. Explain the policy lesson for productivity policy and why human capital is described as crucial.

**Question 5**: Nguyen and Pham (Asian-Pacific Economic Literature, 2018) examined the links between economic growth, inequality and poverty reduction in Vietnam. Explain why extreme poverty is especially sensitive to inequality, and what this implies about the limits of a pure growth strategy.

**Question 6**: Nguyen and Pham (Asian-Pacific Economic Literature, 2018) examined the links between economic growth, inequality and poverty reduction in Vietnam. Explain the policy lesson for making growth more inclusive and one reason the finding is significant for exam-question design.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — The Impact of GET Ahead Training on Empowerment of Female Microfinance Borrowers in Northern Vietnam (Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen 2019)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l52-huis-2019-get-training-microfinance-vietnam]].

- Cluster RCT, intention-to-treat (ITT) combined with DiD, measured at multiple time points — the GET Ahead program, northern Vietnam.
- Training raises perceived self-control, increases decision power over large expenditures, reduces household conflict.
- Effects are only CLEAR at endline (12 months), NOT at midline — empowerment accumulates gradually over time.

### Question 2 — Fostering Conditions for Digital Entrepreneurship: The Role of Institutional Entrepreneurs in FinTech Ecosystem Development (Stein, Evers and O'Gorman 2026)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l55-stein-2026-digital-entrepreneurship-fintech-denmark]].

- Longitudinal qualitative case study — in-depth interviews, field observation, secondary data, the Danish FinTech ecosystem.
- Three necessary conditions: incumbent-startup collaboration, regulation that allows innovation, strong international connections.
- Collective action and NEUTRAL LEADERSHIP sustain these institutional changes over time.

### Question 3 — Estimation of Technical Change and TFP Growth Based on Observable Technology Shifters (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2020)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l61-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-tfp-technology-shifters]].

- Infrastructure + finance enable INVESTMENT; technology creates new production methods; but human capital (education, health) is what CONVERTS technology adoption into actual productivity.
- These factors are COMPLEMENTARY, not independent — without human capital, technology investment struggles to translate into TFP.
- Limitation: the unexplained time trend remains the LARGEST component; panel estimates mainly show ASSOCIATIONS rather than clean causal effects — this may reflect measurement issues more than genuine technological regress.

### Question 4 — Estimation of Technical Change and TFP Growth Based on Observable Technology Shifters (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2020)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l61-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-tfp-technology-shifters]].

- Policy: productivity policy should jointly improve infrastructure, financial access, R&amp;D/digital technology, education and health — not treat factors in isolation.
- Human capital is crucial because it turns IMPORTED or DOMESTIC technology into actual productivity — prioritize health/education over pure R&amp;D/infrastructure spending alone.
- A paper by Prof. Heshmati himself, opening LN6 — directly connects to the middle-income-trap and convergence debate in LN1.

### Question 5 — Economic Growth, Inequality and Poverty in Vietnam (Nguyen and Pham 2018)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]].

- Growth raises AVERAGE income, but distribution determines WHO receives those gains.
- The POOREST households are especially sensitive to inequality because even strong national growth may BYPASS them if gains concentrate among the better-off.
- Growth can be pro-poor in an ABSOLUTE sense while the rich still gain FASTER — a complete assessment must examine both poverty reduction AND inequality change.

### Question 6 — Economic Growth, Inequality and Poverty in Vietnam (Nguyen and Pham 2018)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]].

- Policy: maintain growth but make it more inclusive through regional infrastructure, education, health, productive opportunities, targeted support for ethnic minorities/remote areas.
- This is the paper behind BOTH the K30 exam (question 6) AND the K31 exam (question 2) — two consecutive cohorts — ⭐⭐ the single most revision-worthy paper of the 20.
- Poverty remains persistently concentrated among ethnic minorities/remote mountainous communities despite rapid growth — supplementary evidence for the LN1 middle-income-trap debate.
