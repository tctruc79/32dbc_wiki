## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Jiang, Levine and Lin (Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 2019) examined the effect of bank competition on liquidity creation. Discuss their findings concerning the effect of deregulation-driven bank competition on liquidity creation.

**Question 2**: Cai and Le (Resources Policy, 2023) examined the roles of natural resources, financial development and CSR in Vietnam's green growth. Discuss their findings concerning how natural resources, financial development and CSR relate to green growth in Vietnam in the short run versus the long run.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Tran et al. (Environmental Challenges, 2022) examined the livelihood vulnerability of rice farmers in North-Central Vietnam under climate change. Explain the counterintuitive finding that formal credit and irrigation access INCREASE vulnerability, and why vulnerability differs across households facing the same hazard.

**Question 4**: Tran et al. (Environmental Challenges, 2022) examined the livelihood vulnerability of rice farmers in North-Central Vietnam under climate change. Explain the policy recommendations and one qualification about generalizing a single-hazard explanation of vulnerability.

**Question 5**: Hastuti et al. (World Development Perspectives, 2025) examined how climate variability affects labor mobility among Indonesian agricultural households. Explain why rainfall and temperature operate through different channels, and why the response differs between educated farmers and smallholders.

**Question 6**: Hastuti et al. (World Development Perspectives, 2025) examined how climate variability affects labor mobility among Indonesian agricultural households. Explain the policy implication and one limitation regarding whether this mobility represents successful adaptation.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — Competition and Bank Liquidity Creation (Jiang, Levine and Lin 2019)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l32-jiang-2019-competition-bank-liquidity-creation]].

- DiD exploiting waves of US interstate bank deregulation; OLS with state-year and bank fixed effects.
- Increased bank competition → DECREASED liquidity creation, consistent across every measure used.
- The effect is stronger for low-profit/weak-capital banks — rejecting the "competition spurs innovation → more liquidity" hypothesis.

### Question 2 — Natural Resources and Financial Development: The Role of CSR in Vietnam's Green Growth (Cai and Le 2023)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l34-cai-2023-natural-resources-financial-development-vietnam]].

- PMG-ARDL, Vietnamese time series <span class="stat">1999–2018</span>; Johansen-Fisher/Kao cointegration tests; VECM; Wald test.
- Resources + financial development + CSR are all POSITIVE with green growth in the LONG RUN.
- But all three are NEGATIVE with green growth in the SHORT RUN — a clear reversal.

### Question 3 — Climate Change and Livelihood Vulnerability of Rice Farmers in North-Central Vietnam (Tran et al. 2022)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l45-tran-2022-rice-farmers-vulnerability-nghean]].

- The LVI separates exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity — explaining why households facing similar floods/droughts suffer very different welfare losses.
- Credit/irrigation INCREASE vulnerability — possibly because borrowing households intensify production and are more exposed to crop failure, or reverse causality (already-vulnerable households need credit/irrigation more).
- COVID-19 weakened markets/social networks, and poorly-adapted irrigation sometimes increased losses — climatic, institutional, and economic shocks COMPOUND.

### Question 4 — Climate Change and Livelihood Vulnerability of Rice Farmers in North-Central Vietnam (Tran et al. 2022)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l45-tran-2022-rice-farmers-vulnerability-nghean]].

- Policy: strengthen irrigation/extension/climate training, improve farmer-cooperative-government links, provide affordable formal credit + input support.
- Limitation: NO single hazard fully explains vulnerability — households face several interacting stresses that differ across locations/social groups.
- Sample of <span class="stat">396</span> households, 3 coastal districts of Nghe An — results are locally specific, caution needed extrapolating nationally.

### Question 5 — Climate Change and Labor Mobility: Agricultural Households in Indonesia (Hastuti et al. 2025)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l46-hastuti-2025-climate-labor-mobility-indonesia]].

- Farm production cost is a significant mediating channel for RAINFALL's effect, but NOT for TEMPERATURE's effect — two different transmission mechanisms.
- More educated households have more non-farm job options (pull); smallholders have less capacity to absorb agricultural losses (push).
- Labor mobility can be a PROACTIVE adaptation strategy, but it may also be a distress response FORCED by hardship — not always a positive choice.

### Question 6 — Climate Change and Labor Mobility: Agricultural Households in Indonesia (Hastuti et al. 2025)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l46-hastuti-2025-climate-labor-mobility-indonesia]].

- Policy: combine climate-resilient/precision agriculture with education, vocational skills, and cross-institutional coordination, so mobility becomes a productive choice rather than a desperate response.
- Limitation: the study observes exit from agriculture more clearly than any later RETURN — long-run outcomes for movers are not tracked.
- A complementary contrast with L45 (adapting IN PLACE) — same climate pressure, different response depending on household resources.
