## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Tran et al. (Environmental Challenges, 2022) examined the livelihood vulnerability of rice farmers in North-Central Vietnam under climate change. Discuss their findings concerning the level and sources of livelihood vulnerability among rice farmers in Nghe An.

**Question 2**: Hastuti et al. (World Development Perspectives, 2025) examined how climate variability affects labor mobility among Indonesian agricultural households. Discuss their findings concerning how rainfall and temperature variability affect the probability of Indonesian households shifting labor out of agriculture.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen (World Development, 2019) examined the impact of the GET Ahead training programme on female microfinance borrowers' empowerment. Explain why daily spending decisions show less improvement than major decisions, and why inviting husbands did not add measurable benefit.

**Question 4**: Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen (World Development, 2019) examined the impact of the GET Ahead training programme on female microfinance borrowers' empowerment. Explain the policy lesson for microfinance program design and one limitation of the study's ability to isolate training's effect.

**Question 5**: Stein, Evers and O'Gorman (Technovation, 2026) examined the role of institutional entrepreneurs in Denmark's FinTech ecosystem development. Explain the causal logic connecting institutional entrepreneurs to ecosystem formation, and why regulation is a double-edged factor.

**Question 6**: Stein, Evers and O'Gorman (Technovation, 2026) examined the role of institutional entrepreneurs in Denmark's FinTech ecosystem development. Explain the policy lesson for building a digital entrepreneurial ecosystem and how Vietnam might apply it.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — Climate Change and Livelihood Vulnerability of Rice Farmers in North-Central Vietnam (Tran et al. 2022)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l45-tran-2022-rice-farmers-vulnerability-nghean]].

- Livelihood Vulnerability Index (LVI) + an IPCC-framework variant, correlation matrix + beta regression, rice-farming households in coastal Nghe An.
- <span class="stat">76%</span> of surveyed households are only SLIGHTLY vulnerable, retaining coping capacity.
- Floods, droughts, irrigation conditions, institutional support, and household characteristics all significantly shape vulnerability.

### Question 2 — Climate Change and Labor Mobility: Agricultural Households in Indonesia (Hastuti et al. 2025)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l46-hastuti-2025-climate-labor-mobility-indonesia]].

- Exogenous instrumental variables (altitude for temperature, latitude for rainfall), 2SLS estimation on an Indonesian household panel, combined with mediation analysis.
- Rainfall +1% raises the labor-shift probability by <span class="stat">0.47 pp</span>; temperature +1% by <span class="stat">1.38 pp</span> — both statistically significant.
- The response is stronger in Java, among more educated farmers, and among smaller landholders.

### Question 3 — The Impact of GET Ahead Training on Empowerment of Female Microfinance Borrowers in Northern Vietnam (Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen 2019)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l52-huis-2019-get-training-microfinance-vietnam]].

- Women already managed most ROUTINE spending beforehand — little room for improvement; MAJOR decisions reveal the real shift in household bargaining power.
- Microcredit provides RESOURCES but does not automatically create control — training adds skills, gender awareness, and confidence that convert resources into real agency.
- Inviting husbands created NO significant difference — possibly due to low actual participation rates; sensitive household conflict may also be under-reported.

### Question 4 — The Impact of GET Ahead Training on Empowerment of Female Microfinance Borrowers in Northern Vietnam (Huis, Lensink, Vu and Hansen 2019)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l52-huis-2019-get-training-microfinance-vietnam]].

- Policy: dedicated skills/agency training for female microfinance borrowers is an effective tool; longer-term (not just midline) evaluation is needed to see the true effect.
- Limitation: the design identifies the effect of TRAINING, not microcredit in general — cannot be generalized to all credit programs without training.
- Provides stronger causal evidence than L51 (same theme of women's economic empowerment) thanks to the RCT design.

### Question 5 — Fostering Conditions for Digital Entrepreneurship: The Role of Institutional Entrepreneurs in FinTech Ecosystem Development (Stein, Evers and O'Gorman 2026)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l55-stein-2026-digital-entrepreneurship-fintech-denmark]].

- Startups need knowledge, legitimacy, customers, and regulatory clarity — things no single firm can build alone.
- Institutional entrepreneurs connect fragmented actors, negotiate rules, build shared platforms — turning isolated firms into an ecosystem.
- Regulation both PROTECTS market trust and can FAVOR incumbents while BLOCKING new entry — this balance shifts as the ecosystem matures.

### Question 6 — Fostering Conditions for Digital Entrepreneurship: The Role of Institutional Entrepreneurs in FinTech Ecosystem Development (Stein, Evers and O'Gorman 2026)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l55-stein-2026-digital-entrepreneurship-fintech-denmark]].

- Policy: create neutral collaborative platforms, regulatory sandboxes, international knowledge networks.
- Vietnam can apply this logic but must adapt it to its own financial-institution and regulatory capacity — not simply copy the Danish model.
- The same "institutions/behavior matter more than technology" theme reappears in L93/L95 (AI & digitalization in Vietnam) — technology alone is not enough without matching institutions.
