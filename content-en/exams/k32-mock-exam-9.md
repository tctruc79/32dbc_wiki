## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2024) examined AI development in Vietnam's energy and economic systems. Discuss their findings concerning Vietnam's AI investment and research standing relative to regional peers.

**Question 2**: Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong (International Journal of Information Management Data Insights, 2024) examined the barriers to construction digitalization in Vietnam. Discuss their findings concerning the coverage of barriers to construction digitalization and the balance between intrinsic and impact-related barriers.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Sheng, Song and Zheng (Journal of International Money and Finance, 2025) examined how Chinese exporters responded to the US-China trade war. Explain the mechanism driving China's preference for vertical over horizontal diversion, and why total exports stayed resilient.

**Question 4**: Sheng, Song and Zheng (Journal of International Money and Finance, 2025) examined how Chinese exporters responded to the US-China trade war. Explain the policy implication for trade-war analysis and one limitation of generalizing beyond the study period.

**Question 5**: Toai (Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 2025) examined the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's imports and exports. Explain the two structural risks the author identifies alongside Vietnam's trade gains.

**Question 6**: Toai (Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 2025) examined the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's imports and exports. Explain the four recommendations for converting this opportunity into sustainable competitiveness, and one limitation of the study's method.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — AI Development in Vietnam's Energy and Economic Systems: A Critical Review (Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong 2024)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l93-pham-2024-ai-development-vietnam-review]].

- A critical review (no own empirical dataset), benchmarking the OECD.AI database + policy documents against major regional/global markets.
- Vietnam invested only <span class="stat">USD 31m</span> in AI in 2021 — far below Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Malaysia, on par only with the Philippines.
- The 2021 national strategy targets five reputable AI brands + a top-5 ASEAN position by 2025, yet by end-2023 NOT ONE brand had been identified.

### Question 2 — Investigating Potential Barriers to Construction Digitalization in Emerging Economies (Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong 2024)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]].

- Mixed methods: qualitative arm (literature review, pilot testing) + quantitative arm (Likert survey, exploratory factor analysis, one-way ANOVA).
- A survey of <span class="stat">248</span> experts (clients, contractors, consultants) in Vietnamese construction, evaluating <span class="stat">31</span> potential barriers.
- The BIGGEST barrier: social and habitual resistance to change — standing out clearly ahead of cost, data, cybersecurity, and standards barriers.

### Question 3 — How did Chinese Exporters Manage the Trade War? (Sheng, Song and Zheng 2025)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]].

- Moving high-quality goods to the South AVOIDS head-on competition with high-quality Northern rivals and LOWERS the cost of entering poorer markets.
- The quality-UPGRADING effect always DOMINATES the price-depressing effect — China's gross FOB price in third markets still RISES even as quality-adjusted prices fall.
- Exports to the US fell but exports to the rest of the world rose ALMOST OFFSETTING it — competition SHIFTS rather than disappears.

### Question 4 — How did Chinese Exporters Manage the Trade War? (Sheng, Song and Zheng 2025)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l104-sheng-2025-chinese-exporters-trade-diversion]].

- Policy: trade policy must account for firms' ability to REDIRECT and UPGRADE products — not assume tariffs simply reduce bilateral trade.
- Limitation: evidence is mainly from the PRE-pandemic period — this does not mean trade diversion is costless; firms still bear different price/standards/market risks.
- Vietnam is excluded from the sample for robustness — showing Vietnam's export growth (L107) more likely reflects the US DIRECTLY SUBSTITUTING Chinese supply, not Vietnam acting as a transshipment point.

### Question 5 — Impact of the US-China Trade War on Vietnam's Imports and Exports (Toai 2025)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l107-toai-2025-vietnam-import-export-trade-war]].

- Risk 1 — vertical specialization: export growth is TIGHTLY BOUND to Chinese input imports — export performance stays exposed to a SINGLE supplier without domestic supporting industries.
- Risk 2 — a ballooning surplus and regulatory scrutiny: exports to the US are almost 5× imports from the US; the overlap between Chinese imports and US exports has drawn US Customs suspicion of transshipment.
- The "export win" may reflect OPPORTUNISTIC production relocation rather than genuine domestic competitiveness.

### Question 6 — Impact of the US-China Trade War on Vietnam's Imports and Exports (Toai 2025)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l107-toai-2025-vietnam-import-export-trade-war]].

- Policy: diversify input sourcing; strengthen domestic supporting industries; move up the value-added ladder (assembly → design/branding); rebalance trade diplomacy.
- Limitation: this is a DESCRIPTIVE study, and the analysis period overlaps COVID-19 — not every trade change can be causally attributed to the trade war alone.
- Together with L104, closes LN10 — complementing L104's tight short-run DiD evidence with a broader but less causally rigorous long-run descriptive picture.
