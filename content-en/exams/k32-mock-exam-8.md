## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Saidani et al. (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2019) examined a taxonomy of circular economy indicators. Discuss their findings concerning the coverage of the three circularity loops and the balance between intrinsic circularity and sustainability impact.

**Question 2**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2021) examined the determinants of the urban circular economy in Sweden. Discuss their findings concerning the determinants of the Circular Economy Index across Swedish municipalities.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2024) examined AI development in Vietnam's energy and economic systems. Explain the "quantity-quality paradox" in Vietnam's AI research output, and why the gap is institutional as well as technological.

**Question 4**: Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2024) examined AI development in Vietnam's energy and economic systems. Explain the policy priorities recommended and one reason the current low application level should not be read only as failure.

**Question 5**: Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong (International Journal of Information Management Data Insights, 2024) examined the barriers to construction digitalization in Vietnam. Explain why Vietnam's barrier profile differs from international comparators, and how the barriers reinforce one another.

**Question 6**: Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong (International Journal of Information Management Data Insights, 2024) examined the barriers to construction digitalization in Vietnam. Explain the policy recommendation and one qualification about applying a single national policy to all stakeholder groups.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — A Taxonomy of Circular Economy Indicators (Saidani et al. 2019)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l85-saidani-2019-taxonomy-circular-economy-indicators]].

- Systematic review of academic + non-academic sources, classifying 55 indicators into a 10-category taxonomy (level, loop, performance, perspective...).
- <span class="stat">90%</span> of micro-level indicators cover the recycling loop but only <span class="stat">45%</span> cover all three loops in one consistent set.
- <span class="stat">80%</span> measure INTRINSIC circularity, only <span class="stat">40%</span> measure sustainability IMPACT, just <span class="stat">20%</span> measure BOTH.

### Question 2 — Assessment of the Urban Circular Economy in Sweden (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2021)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l86-heshmati-rashidghalam-2021-urban-circular-economy-sweden]].

- PCA (weighted average of components with eigenvalue &gt;1) → one composite circular-economy index, FE panel, <span class="stat">273</span> Swedish municipalities (<span class="stat">2012–2018</span>).
- The index varies widely — sparsely-populated peripheral municipalities perform BEST; Stockholm (the most developed capital) is LOWEST.
- High population density and high unemployment significantly reduce the index; the waste-collection fee is the strongest POSITIVE lever (+1 SEK/m² → index <span class="stat">+0.50</span>).

### Question 3 — AI Development in Vietnam's Energy and Economic Systems: A Critical Review (Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong 2024)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l93-pham-2024-ai-development-vietnam-review]].

- Quantity-quality paradox: LOW publication volume but a top-5%-journal share ranking SECOND in the region — good individual quality, weak systemic scale.
- Lacking computing infrastructure, quality data, skilled researchers, finance, and clear priorities → AI projects remain ISOLATED, unable to scale into productivity/energy-efficiency gains.
- Vietnam's problem is STRATEGIC ALLOCATION, not simply AI adoption — limited resources require investment, regulation, research, and infrastructure to follow one national direction.

### Question 4 — AI Development in Vietnam's Energy and Economic Systems: A Critical Review (Pham, Nong, Simshauser, Nguyen and Duong 2024)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l93-pham-2024-ai-development-vietnam-review]].

- Policy: choose priority sectors, invest in data/computing infrastructure, fund applied research, link ministries-universities-firms, deepen international cooperation, emphasize clean energy/agriculture.
- Limitation: AI application in energy is still LOW globally, not just in Vietnam — this also represents future OPPORTUNITY, not only current failure.
- The paper that opens and frames LN9's digitalization cluster — echoing the same "institutions matter more than technology" theme as L55.

### Question 5 — Investigating Potential Barriers to Construction Digitalization in Emerging Economies (Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong 2024)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]].

- Vietnam's barrier profile differs markedly from comparators — social resistance ranks FIRST in Vietnam but is NOT identified as a notable barrier in several other countries.
- Construction digitalization is both an ORGANIZATIONAL/BEHAVIORAL problem and a technological one — not simply a lack of tools.
- Barriers reinforce each other: weak skills reduce trust, missing data/standards weaken interoperability, security concerns raise investment risk — a coordinated package is needed, not one subsidy.

### Question 6 — Investigating Potential Barriers to Construction Digitalization in Emerging Economies (Nguyen Van Tam, Nguyen Quoc Toan and Vu Van Phong 2024)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l95-tam-2024-construction-digitalization-barriers-vietnam]].

- Policy: combine digital-skills/change-management programs with lower technology costs, better data/cybersecurity systems, clear industry standards, government-firm-education cooperation.
- Limitation: stakeholder groups (clients/contractors/consultants) rank barriers SIGNIFICANTLY DIFFERENTLY — one policy CANNOT solve every adoption problem.
- ⭐⭐ A trunk paper — already asked on K31 (question 3), still on the K32 shortlist; shares the "institutions/behavior > technology" theme with L93, L55, L25.
