# K32 Mock Exam — Set 6 (LN6 compulsory + LN7 elective)

Set 6: compulsory questions 1–2 use L61 + L64 (LN6, method + main-findings angle); elective questions 3–6 use L71 + L73 (LN7, two angles each: mechanism/robustness + policy implications). ## Exam Rules &amp; Grading Structure

**120 minutes, closed-book**, modeled exactly on the real K31 exam rules. 2 compulsory questions (Q1–2) are both mandatory; 4 elective questions (Q3–6), choose at least 2 — if more than 2 are answered, only the best 2 scores count. 12.5 points each, 50 points per set.

## Questions

### A. Compulsory questions (each 12.5 points)

**Question 1**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Productivity Analysis, 2020) examined technical change and TFP growth based on observable technology shifters. Discuss their findings concerning how observable technology shifters explain TFP growth compared to an unobserved time trend alone.

**Question 2**: Nguyen and Pham (Asian-Pacific Economic Literature, 2018) examined the links between economic growth, inequality and poverty reduction in Vietnam. Discuss their findings concerning how growth and inequality jointly affected poverty reduction in Vietnam across two periods.


### B. Elective questions (choose a minimum of 2 questions, each 12.5 points)

**Question 3**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Infrastructure Systems, 2020) examined the measurement of urban infrastructure and its effects on urbanization in China. Explain the policy warning that follows from the time trends of the individual sub-indices, and why using PCA rather than direct regression matters here.

**Question 4**: Heshmati and Rashidghalam (Journal of Infrastructure Systems, 2020) examined the measurement of urban infrastructure and its effects on urbanization in China. Explain the policy recommendation for Vietnam and one limitation of the statistical relationships identified.

**Question 5**: McGuinness, Kelly, Pham, Ha and Whelan (World Development, 2021) examined the changing returns to education in Vietnam. Explain why a falling education premium does not necessarily mean education is worthless, and the exception found for women.

**Question 6**: McGuinness, Kelly, Pham, Ha and Whelan (World Development, 2021) examined the changing returns to education in Vietnam. Explain the policy lesson to avoid a low-skill middle-income trap and one qualification about the demand estimates.

## Detailed Answers

### Question 1 — Estimation of Technical Change and TFP Growth Based on Observable Technology Shifters (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2020)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l61-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-tfp-technology-shifters]].

- A translog production function extended with observable technology shifters, a panel of <span class="stat">190</span> countries 1996–2013.
- The human-capital index has the highest elasticity (<span class="stat">0.234</span>); the technology index (R&amp;D, high-tech exports, patents) is slightly NEGATIVE (<span class="stat">−0.043</span>).
- Technical change and TFP growth are NEGATIVE across ALL income groups — the paper's most counterintuitive finding.

### Question 2 — Economic Growth, Inequality and Poverty in Vietnam (Nguyen and Pham 2018)

*Compulsory · Method + main findings*

See in full: [[l64-nguyen-pham-2018-growth-inequality-poverty-vietnam]].

- Foster-Greer-Thorbecke + Datt-Ravallion decomposition (separating growth from inequality) + the Kakwani-Pernia pro-poor index, Vietnamese household surveys 1993–2008.
- 1993–98: inequality ROSE, the pro-poor index was only <span class="stat">0.90</span>.
- 2004–08: inequality FELL, the index jumped to <span class="stat">1.80</span> ("highly pro-poor") — Vietnamese poverty is now MORE sensitive to inequality than to growth.

### Question 3 — Measurement and Analysis of Urban Infrastructure and its Effects on Urbanization in China (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2020)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l71-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-urban-infrastructure-china]].

- The 74 indicators are highly intercorrelated — direct regression would face severe multicollinearity; PCA separates them into components internally correlated but mutually uncorrelated.
- The sharpest warning: exactly the 3 components with the STRONGEST coefficients (employment, security, technology) DECLINED over 2005–2014.
- China is letting exactly the most effective infrastructure pillars erode — a paradox worth flagging for policy.

### Question 4 — Measurement and Analysis of Urban Infrastructure and its Effects on Urbanization in China (Heshmati and Rashidghalam 2020)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l71-heshmati-rashidghalam-2020-urban-infrastructure-china]].

- Policy: central government should improve resource allocation between rich/poor provinces; each province needs its own urbanization plan; highly urbanized provinces should build "smart/green cities".
- Limitation: the estimates identify STATISTICAL relationships more clearly than CAUSALITY, and not every component is significant in every specification.
- Already asked on the real K31 exam (question 6), still on the K32 shortlist — ⭐⭐ a trunk paper, high revision priority.

### Question 5 — Returns to Education in Vietnam: A Changing Landscape (McGuinness, Kelly, Pham, Ha and Whelan 2021)

*Elective · Mechanism / robustness*

See in full: [[l73-mcguinness-2021-returns-to-education-vietnam]].

- The premium depends on labor DEMAND, not just supply — a falling premium may reflect rising graduate supply, weak skilled-job creation, or skill-job mismatch.
- More graduates increasingly work in LOWER-skill jobs — supporting the view that program relevance and job structure are the real issue, not education itself.
- Exception: demand for FEMALE postgraduate labor stayed POSITIVE throughout — a similar but more volatile pattern than for men.

### Question 6 — Returns to Education in Vietnam: A Changing Landscape (McGuinness, Kelly, Pham, Ha and Whelan 2021)

*Elective · Policy implications / critique*

See in full: [[l73-mcguinness-2021-returns-to-education-vietnam]].

- Policy: ensure high-quality FDI keeps flowing in to sustain skilled employment; reform tertiary/vocational education to match real labor-market demand.
- Limitation: the relative-demand estimate depends on assumptions about how easily firms SUBSTITUTE skilled for less-skilled workers.
- The most Vietnam-focused paper of LN7, warning of a "low-skill trap" — adding a new mechanism (skilled labor supply-demand imbalance) to the middle-income-trap debate from LN1.
