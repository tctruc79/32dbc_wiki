## Abstract

> The transition to sustainable economic systems demands an integrated understanding of how
> innovation drives circular economy practices aligned with the United Nations Sustainable
> Development Goals (SDGs). This study presents a bibliometric and conceptual analysis of 1630
> peer-reviewed articles indexed in Scopus between 2020 and 2024, examining the convergence of
> innovation, circular economy, and sustainable development. Using co-occurrence analysis, text
> mining, and bibliographic coupling, the research identifies key thematic structures and global
> collaboration patterns. Findings highlight innovation as a central node linking technological
> and organisational solutions to sustainability frameworks. Thematic clusters show discursive
> maturity around "innovation," "sustainable development," and "circular economy," while text
> mining reveals emerging focuses such as green innovation, technological change, and economic
> growth. Bibliographic coupling underscores a multipolar landscape of scientific production, with
> China, the United Kingdom, and India as major nodes. [...] we show that innovation operates as
> an integrative mechanism with uneven territorial expression and clear implications for theory,
> policy and future research.

**Summary (paraphrase)**: A **bibliometric + conceptual analysis** paper (not original empirical
research) on **1630 Scopus articles, 2020–2024**, at the intersection of **innovation, circular
economy (CE), and SDGs (Sustainable Development Goals)**. Using 3 techniques: keyword
co-occurrence analysis, text mining on titles/abstracts, and bibliographic coupling across
countries.

## Research Questions

The paper explicitly states **3 research questions**: **RQ1** — How is the concept of innovation
articulated with CE in the scientific literature, and what role does it play in achieving the
SDGs? **RQ2** — How are scientific discourses on innovation in sustainability evolving, and what
emerging role do CE and energy transition play in the SDG framework? **RQ3** — How do patterns of
bibliographic coupling between countries influence the formation of common scientific agendas on
innovation, CE, and SDGs?

## Research Framework

CE is defined as the transition from an "extract-make-dispose" model to a
**restorative/regenerative** approach, tightly linked to 3 specific SDGs: **SDG 9** (Industry,
innovation and infrastructure), **SDG 12** (Responsible consumption and production), **SDG 13**
(Climate action). Innovation is treated as a **multifaceted integrative mechanism** — not just
technological but also organizational/policy/social-behavioral; the literature distinguishes
**incremental** vs. **transformative** innovation — the latter creating ruptures in existing
socio-technical regimes. The post-2020 context (COVID-19, digital acceleration, geopolitical
instability) is cited as a catalyst for growing academic interest in the innovation-CE-SDG
intersection.

## Data

**1630 peer-reviewed articles** in Scopus, published 2020–2024, at the intersection of
innovation × circular economy × SDGs (the specific search/filter criteria are not visible in the
extractable content — truncated).

## Methodology

**A mixed-methods strategy**: bibliometric techniques + content analysis assisted by specialized
software, structured around 3 analytical lines matching the 3 RQs: (1) **co-occurrence analysis
of indexed keywords** → identifying thematic clusters + semantic relationships (answering RQ1);
(2) **text mining** on titles/abstracts → examining discursive evolution + linguistic
trajectories (answering RQ2); (3) **analysis of shared-reference connections between countries**
(bibliographic coupling) → identifying scientific communities + emerging international
collaboration patterns (answering RQ3).

## Key Findings

- **Innovation is the central node** linking technological and organisational solutions to
  sustainability frameworks — thematic clusters show "discursive maturity" around the 3 concepts
  "innovation," "sustainable development," "circular economy."
- **Text mining reveals emerging themes**: green innovation, technological change, economic
  growth — showing the literature is expanding beyond core CE/SDG concepts.
- **Bibliographic coupling: a multipolar scientific landscape** — **China, the United Kingdom,
  and India** are the 3 main nodes of global scientific production on this topic — no single
  scientific center, reflecting the geographic dispersion of innovation-CE-SDG research.
- **3 main contributions** (self-stated): (1) an integrated bibliometric synthesis
  (co-occurrence + text mining + bibliographic coupling) revealing the consolidation of
  innovation as a cross-cutting driver in the CE-SDG literature; (2) an explanatory lens
  specifying how innovation mediates between capabilities, institutional coordination, and
  territorial context; (3) identifying a polycentric yet uneven epistemic landscape, deriving
  policy-relevant implications and a targeted future-research agenda.

## Conclusion

The study provides a comprehensive picture of the evolution and current configuration of the
academic field focused on the innovation-CE-SDG intersection, via a combined method of keyword
co-occurrence analysis, text mining, and country-level bibliographic coupling on a corpus of 1630
Scopus articles 2020–2024 — enabling the field's conceptual structure to be mapped and its
discourse tracked (the conclusion is truncated in the extracted content — "...track the
discursive..."). The Discussion section emphasizes innovation is repositioned as a "systemic
enabler" of CE and sustainability transformation, while providing practical guidance for
policymakers/businesses seeking to operationalize CE principles.
