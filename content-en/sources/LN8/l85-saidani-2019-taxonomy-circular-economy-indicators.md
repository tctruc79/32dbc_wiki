# L85 — Saidani, M., Yannou, B., Leroy, Y., Cluzel, F. & Kendall, A. (2019), Journal of Cleaner Production 207: 542–559
> ⭐ ⭐ **ON THE OFFICIAL K32 SHORTLIST** — this is one of the 20 papers Prof. Heshmati fixed on 28/8/2026 as the question scope for the 06/9 written exam. This paper is **new relative to the K31 shortlist**, and has already been asked on a real exam (K30·Q2). See [[k32-shortlist-2026]].


**Authors**: Michael Saidani (corresponding author), Bernard Yannou, Yann Leroy, François Cluzel
(Laboratoire Génie Industriel, CentraleSupélec, Université Paris-Saclay, France), Alissa Kendall
(Dept. of Civil and Environmental Engineering, University of California, Davis, USA). DOI:
10.1016/j.jclepro.2018.10.014. Received 29/6/2018, accepted 3/10/2018.

## Abstract

> Implementing circular economy (CE) principles is increasingly recommended as a convenient
> solution to meet the goals of sustainable development. [...] a wide range of circularity
> indicators (C-indicators) has been developed in recent years. Yet, as there is not one single
> definition of the CE concept, it is of the utmost importance to know what the available
> indicators measure in order to use them properly. Indeed, through a systematic literature review
> [...] 55 sets of C-indicators, developed by scholars, consulting companies and governmental
> agencies, have been identified [...]. Inspired by existing taxonomies of eco-design tools and
> sustainability indicators, and in line with the CE characteristics, a classification of
> indicators aiming to assess, improve, monitor and communicate on the CE performance is proposed
> and discussed. In the developed taxonomy including 10 categories, C-indicators are differentiated
> regarding criteria such as the levels of CE implementation (e.g. micro, meso, macro), the CE
> loops (maintain, reuse, remanufacture, recycle), the performance (intrinsic, impacts), the
> perspective of circularity (actual, potential) [...], or their degree of transversality (generic,
> sector-specific). [...] this study can be experienced on several use cases.

**Summary (paraphrase)**: A **systematic literature review** identifying **55 existing sets of
circularity indicators (C-indicators)** (academic + consulting + government agencies), then
building a **10-category taxonomy** to classify/select the appropriate indicator — it does not
propose a new CE definition, but instead addresses CE's definitional plurality (114 definitions,
Kirchherr et al. 2017) by accepting that plurality and providing a flexible selection tool (an
Excel-based "C-Indicators Advisor").

## Research Questions

With numerous C-indicators developed but no unified CE definition, how can scholars/
businesses/policy-makers be helped to **select the right indicator** for their specific needs
(what to measure, at what level, for what purpose)? This is a pragmatic question quite different
from the approach of [[l84-garcia-barragan-2019-defining-measuring-circular-economy]] (a single
definition) or [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] (measuring barriers with one
composite definition).

## Research Framework

CE definition used: the Kirchherr et al. (2017) synthesis of 114 existing definitions —
operating at 3 levels: micro (products/companies/consumers), meso (eco-industrial parks), macro
(cities/regions/nations). The classification framework draws inspiration from existing
sustainability-indicator taxonomies (EEA 2003: descriptive/performance/efficiency/
policy-effectiveness/welfare) and eco-design-tool taxonomies (Rousseaux et al. 2017: 629 tools,
22 categories). CE rests on 3 EMF (2013) principles: (i) design out waste/pollution; (ii) keep
products/materials at their highest use; (iii) regenerate natural ecosystems.

## Data

**Systematic + extended literature review** (section 3): a keyword search combining "circular
economy," "circularity," "evaluation," "assessment," "measure," "indicators," "indices,"
"metrics" across academic databases (Science Direct, SAGE, Springer, Taylor & Francis, Wiley,
Emerald, JSTOR, Google Scholar) AND non-academic sources (organization reports/websites: Ellen
MacArthur Foundation, European Environmental Agency, European Commission); timeframe 2010–5/2018
(the first C-indicator appeared in 2010), English language, worldwide scope. Result: **55 sets of
C-indicators** from 27 journal articles, 2 conference papers, 1 master's thesis, 7 technical
reports, 12 websites/tools — distribution: 20 sets at the micro level, 16 at meso, 19 at macro
(Tables 4, 5; the full list is in Appendix A with 55 acronyms).

## Methodology

Building a **10-category taxonomy** (Table 3): #1 Levels (micro/meso/macro); #2 Loops (maintain,
reuse/remanufacture, recycle — per the Ellen MacArthur Foundation "butterfly diagram," only the
technical/technosphere branch); #3 Performance (intrinsic vs. impact — measuring inherent
circularity or sustainability consequences); #4 Perspective (potential vs. actual — per Potting
et al. 2016: ex ante/ex durante/ex post); #5 Usages (information, decision-making, communication,
learning); #6 Transversality (generic vs. sector-specific); #7 Dimension (single vs. multiple);
#8 Units (quantitative/qualitative); #9 Format (computational tool vs. manual formula); #10
Sources (academic/business/government). Building a **"C-Indicators Advisor" selection tool**
(Excel-based, 8 input questions) linked to the 55-indicator database.

## Key Findings

- **90% of micro-level indicators include a recycling loop**, but only **45% cover all 3 main
  loops** (maintain/recycle/remanufacture-reuse) within one consistent indicator set — suggesting
  most indicators still skew toward recycling over other CE strategies. Macro-level indicators
  (mostly developed in China) focus on recycling EVEN MORE than micro-level ones.
- **80% of micro-level indicators measure intrinsic circularity**; only **40% measure
  sustainability impact**; only **20% measure BOTH simultaneously** — the social dimension is
  almost entirely neglected relative to economic/environmental (matching Singh et al.'s 2012
  general finding on sustainability indicators).
- **60% of micro-level indicators use only a SINGLE number** to aggregate overall circularity
  performance — controversial since there is no standardized method to combine different CE
  loops into 1 indicator. Only **3/20 micro-level indicators are sector-specific**, the rest are
  generic even though most have only been tested on 1 specific product/sector.
- Micro-level indicators are **45% linked to a computational tool** (more convenient for
  practice), while macro-level indicators are mostly (nearly all 19/19) still in textual/
  manual-formula format.

## Conclusion

The study provides the first "need-driven" 10-category taxonomy for 55 C-indicator sets —
clarifying current ambiguity and supporting appropriate indicator selection via the Excel
"C-Indicators Advisor" tool. Limitations: future research needs to (i) more objectively assess
each indicator's scientific soundness (per EEA 2003, RACER, SMART criteria...); (ii) promote
development of sector-specific indicators to increase practical applicability; (iii) remember
C-indicators are only ONE supporting element in the overall CE transition (needing to be
accompanied by product design, new business models, reverse logistics, and other systemic
conditions — the BS 8001:2017 standard is an example of a complementary tool).

## Relevance to the Course/Vietnam

- **The apex of LN8's CE-measurement debate**: shares the same "114 CE definitions" root
  (Kirchherr et al. 2017) as [[l83-kirchherr-2018-barriers-circular-economy-eu]] and
  [[l84-garcia-barragan-2019-defining-measuring-circular-economy]], but chooses **organized
  pluralism** instead of a single definition/metric — directly opposing L84's ambition for a
  single unambiguous definition. See [[circular-economy]] (Debates section) for the full 3-way
  contrast.
- The finding that "macro-level CE indicators focus on recycling more than micro-level ones, and
  most are developed in China" directly matches the China CE practices described in
  [[l81-su-2013-circular-economy-china]]/[[l82-heshmati-2017-review-circular-economy-implementation]]
  (both NDRC/MEP indicator systems emphasize 3R, especially recycling) — a cross-validation
  across 3 sources.
- Vietnam relevance: when designing a national CE indicator set, Vietnam should avoid repeating
  the "recycling-only" bias this paper identifies (only 45% of global micro-level indicators
  cover all 3 loops) — the "C-Indicators Advisor" tool is a good template for selecting/designing
  indicators fit for Vietnam's context rather than copying another country's indicator set
  wholesale.
