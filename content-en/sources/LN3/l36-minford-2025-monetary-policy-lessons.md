## Abstract

> Monetary developments of recent decades began with much promise with inflation targeting by
> independent central banks; the financial crisis of 2007 ushered in a period of great monetary
> instability. There are lessons for a return to more stability. Central banks need to
> stabilize money supply growth. Fiscal policy should be coopted to a stabilization role to
> reduce interest rate instability, and particularly future risks of hitting the zero-interest
> rate bound. Budget discipline should be enforced by long run solvency rules, not by short run
> fiscal rules that in practice prevent the use of fiscal policy. Nor should the budget be
> burdened by monetary policy methods that transfer seigniorage to commercial banks.

**Summary (interpretation)**: The paper reviews decades of monetary policy developments: a
promising start with inflation targeting by independent central banks, followed by the 2007
financial crisis, which ushered in a period of severe monetary instability. The paper draws
lessons for returning to stability and applies them to the current policy context (high
inflation, near recession).

## Research Questions

After the 2007 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic pushed monetary policy into a period
of severe instability, what lessons can be drawn for a return to long-run monetary stability?

## Research Framework

A policy essay, not empirical research — it synthesizes historical lessons in monetary policy
from the 1970s to the present to offer current policy recommendations. Historical narrative:
chaotic inflation in the 1970s → aggressive monetary tightening in the 1980s → inflation
targeting by independent central banks → the "Great Moderation" → excessive easing in the
2000s → the financial crisis → the zero lower bound → the COVID-19 pandemic → inflation
returning to high levels.

## Key Findings

**Three main lessons for stability**: (1) Central banks need to **stabilize money supply
growth**. (2) **Fiscal policy should be "co-opted" into a stabilizing role** to reduce
interest-rate volatility, particularly to reduce the future risk of hitting the zero lower
bound. (3) **Budget discipline should be enforced through long-run solvency rules**, not
short-run fiscal rules (which in practice tend to obstruct the use of fiscal policy); the
budget also should not be burdened by monetary policy approaches that transfer seigniorage to
commercial banks.

## Conclusion

Monetary policy has tightened significantly to combat high inflation, but should avoid
**over-tightening** now that money supply growth has successfully been brought down to a
non-inflationary level — the risks of a deep recession and of another banking crisis need to
be weighed carefully.

## Relevance to the Course/Vietnam

- A global macro perspective that complements the other empirical papers in LN3 — it provides
  a long-run historical frame (1970s–present) for the specific crises analyzed by L31 (banking)
  and L33 (COVID-19).
- Directly relevant to discussions of Vietnamese monetary policy: balancing inflation control,
  exchange-rate stability, and fiscal space — in particular, the lesson that the budget "should
  not be burdened by seigniorage" connects to discussions of central bank independence in
  developing countries.
