## Abstract

> A decade and a half after the Global Financial Crisis (GFC) and in the aftermath of the
> COVID-19 pandemic, the banking industry faces unprecedented challenges amid shifting
> geopolitical landscapes and looming financial fragmentation. This review paper synthesizes
> existing research on the transformation of banking risks and operations, with a particular
> focus on the adoption of digital technologies and the evolution of regulatory frameworks. It
> critically examines the impact of the GFC, the pandemic and global geopolitical tensions on
> financial stability, drawing on the work of academics and multilateral financial
> institutions. As emerging market banks may be particularly vulnerable to new challenges and
> risks, we also pay special attention to the role of developing market banks in this new era.

**Summary (interpretation)**: A review article synthesizing existing research on the
transformation of banking risk and operations, a decade and a half after the Global Financial
Crisis (GFC) and in the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic, against a backdrop of geopolitical
tension and the risk of global financial fragmentation. Its focus: **digital technology
adoption** and the **evolution of regulatory frameworks**. Particular attention is paid to the
role and vulnerability of banks in emerging/developing markets in the face of the new risks and
challenges of this era.

## Research Questions

How is the banking sector transforming a decade and a half after the GFC and in the aftermath
of the COVID-19 pandemic, against a backdrop of geopolitical tension and the risk of global
financial fragmentation? What risks do emerging/developing-market banks face specifically in
this era?

## Research Framework

A review essay, not original empirical research — it synthesizes work by scholars and
multilateral financial institutions to assess the impact of the GFC, the pandemic, and global
geopolitical tensions on **financial stability**. Two main transformation trends structure the
synthesis: (1) the adoption of digital technology in banking; (2) the evolution of regulatory
frameworks in the post-GFC/pandemic era.

## Key Findings

The banking sector faces "unprecedented" challenges arising from a shifting geopolitical
landscape combined with the risk of financial fragmentation (the global financial system
splitting along geopolitical blocs rather than remaining integrated). Banks in emerging markets
are assessed as **particularly vulnerable** to the new challenges/risks of this era of
geopolitical fragmentation.

## Conclusion

The paper concludes that the banking sector needs to adapt simultaneously to both
transformation trends (digitalization + regulatory evolution) amid rising geopolitical risk,
and emphasizes the need for special attention to the vulnerability of emerging-market banks
when designing global financial-stability policy.
