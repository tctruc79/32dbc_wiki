## Summary

A review article synthesizing existing research on the transformation of banking risk and
operations, a decade and a half after the Global Financial Crisis (GFC) and in the aftermath of
the COVID-19 pandemic, against a backdrop of geopolitical tension and the risk of global
financial fragmentation. Its focus: **digital technology adoption** and the **evolution of
regulatory frameworks**. Particular attention is paid to the role and vulnerability of banks in
emerging/developing markets in the face of the new risks and challenges of this era.

## Research questions & methodology

- A review essay, not original empirical research — it synthesizes work by scholars and
  multilateral financial institutions to assess the impact of the GFC, the pandemic, and global
  geopolitical tensions on **financial stability**.

## Key results

- The banking sector faces "unprecedented" challenges arising from a shifting geopolitical
  landscape combined with the risk of financial fragmentation (the global financial system
  splitting along geopolitical blocs rather than remaining integrated).
- Two main transformation trends are emphasized: (1) the adoption of digital technology in
  banking; (2) the evolution of regulatory frameworks in the post-GFC/pandemic era.
- Banks in emerging markets are assessed as **particularly vulnerable** to the new
  challenges/risks of this era of geopolitical fragmentation.

## Significance for the course/Vietnam

- A broad (macro/policy review) perspective that complements the other empirical papers in LN3
  (L31, L32) — raises the question of how vulnerable Vietnam's banking system (an emerging
  market) is to global financial fragmentation and digitalization pressures.
- Connects to the lecture 9 theme (AI, Digitalization) in the course outline — the intersection
  of digital banking and geopolitics.
