# L33 — Lastunen & Richiardi (2023), World Development Perspectives 30: 100503

**Authors**: Jesse Lastunen (UNU-WIDER, Helsinki), Matteo Richiardi (Centre for
Microsimulation and Policy Analysis, University of Essex).

## Abstract

> We develop a new methodology to nowcast the effects of the COVID-19 crisis on GDP and
> forecast its evolution in small, export-oriented countries. To this aim, we exploit variation
> in financial indexes at the industry level in the early stages of the crisis and relate them
> to the expected duration of the crisis for each industry, under the assumption that the main
> shocks to financial prices in 2020 came from COVID-19. Starting from the latest official
> information available at different stages of the crisis on industry-level trend deviations of
> GDP, often a few months old, we predict the ensuing recovery trajectories using the most
> recent financial data available at the time of the prediction. The financial data reflect,
> among other things, how subsequent waves of infections and information about new vaccines
> have impacted expectations about the future. We apply our method to Vietnam, one of the most
> open economies in the world, and obtain predictions that are more optimistic than
> projections by the International Monetary Fund and other international forecasters, and
> closer to the realised figures. Our claim is that this better-than-expected performance was
> visible in stock market data early on but was largely missed by conventional forecasting
> methods.

**Summary (interpretation)**: The paper develops a new **nowcasting** method (forecasting the
present/very near future) for the impact of COVID-19 on GDP, and forecasts recovery
trajectories for small, export-oriented economies, using **financial data** (sectoral stock
indices) rather than traditional macroeconomic data (which is subject to substantial
publication lags). Applied to Vietnam, the resulting forecasts are **more optimistic and more
accurate** than those of the IMF and other international forecasting institutions.

## Research Questions

Traditional crisis-recovery forecasts (IMF, World Bank, OECD) rely on published macroeconomic
data that lags by months — while policymaking needs earlier information. Question: can
sector-level financial data (which reflects expectations almost instantaneously) be used to
nowcast/forecast GDP recovery more accurately than traditional methods?

## Research Framework

**Core assumption**: fluctuations in sectoral stock indices in 2020 were driven predominantly
by COVID-19 news (not other shocks) — a reasonable assumption given the scale and pervasiveness
of the COVID-19 crisis. Nowcasting framework: exploit sector-level financial index fluctuations
in the early stage of the crisis, linking them to each sector's expected crisis duration;
distinguishes between domestic-facing sectors (domestic firm valuations) and export-facing
sectors (subject to international competitive pressure).

## Data

Stock indices for three Vietnamese sectors (industrials, cyclicals, non-cyclicals),
cross-referenced against the Oxford Government Stringency Index and new COVID-19 case counts,
2018–2021.

## Methodology

Starting from the most recently available sector-level GDP trend deviation (typically lagging
by several months), the latest financial data is used to project the subsequent recovery
trajectory — a nowcasting technique combining dynamic-factor thinking with high-frequency
financial data in place of low-frequency macroeconomic data.

## Regression/Estimation Results — comparison of Vietnam's 2020 GDP forecasts

| Source | Publication date | GDP trend deviation | Corresponding real GDP growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vietnam GSO (official figures)** | 2021 | **−4.0%** | **2.5%** |
| **This paper (Q2/2020 data)** | 09/2020 | **−4.1%** | **2.4%** |
| Vietnamese government (growth target) | 09/2020 | −4.0% to −4.5% | 2.0–2.5% |
| Atradius | 11/2020 | −4.2% | 2.3% |
| ADB | 09/2020 | −4.7% | 1.8% |
| IMF | 10/2020 | −4.9% | 1.6% |

The paper's forecast (based on Q2/2020 data, published 09/2020) is the **closest to the actual
official figure** (GSO) among all forecasts made at the same point in time — more accurate
than the IMF or ADB. When updated with Q3/2020 data (up to 28/12), the estimate narrows to
about <span class="stat">−3.8% to −4.0%</span> (point estimate −3.9%) — even closer to the
final GSO figure (−4.0%).

## Key Findings

Vietnam was one of the few countries to achieve positive nominal GDP growth in 2020 — this
exceptional performance was apparent early in stock market data but was largely missed by
traditional forecasts.

## Conclusion

High-frequency financial data is an effective nowcasting tool for small, export-oriented
economies during a globally pervasive crisis such as COVID-19. Limitation acknowledged by the
authors: only one case study (Vietnam); further testing across other contexts/time frames is
needed for full validation.
