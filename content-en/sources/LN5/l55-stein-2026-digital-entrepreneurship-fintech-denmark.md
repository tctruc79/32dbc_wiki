# L55 — Stein, Evers & O'Gorman (2026), Technovation 150: 103386
> ⭐ ⭐ **ON THE OFFICIAL K32 SHORTLIST** — this is one of the 20 papers Prof. Heshmati fixed on 28/8/2026 as the question scope for the 06/9 written exam. This paper is **new relative to the K31 shortlist**. See [[k32-shortlist-2026]].


**Authors**: Luiza Stein (School of Business, Innovation and Sustainability, Halmstad
University, Sweden), Natasha Evers (Trinity Business School, Dublin), Colm O'Gorman (DCU
Business School, Dublin City University). Part of the "Digital Entrepreneurship" special issue
of *Technovation*.

## Abstract

> This paper explores the role of institutional entrepreneurship in developing Digital
> Entrepreneurial Ecosystems (DEE). With much of the literature on DEE focused on
> organisational-level analysis, there is a need for further research that includes
> system-level analysis, and specifically research that explores how ecosystem actors support
> Digital Entrepreneurship (DE) by facilitating adaptation of the institutional environment. We
> analyse a single longitudinal case study of the Danish FinTech ecosystem, a DEE that
> developed in the context of a traditional, highly regulated industry and a turbulent global
> FinTech scene. We use this case to explore two questions: "what institutional forces
> influence the development of the DEE?", and "how do institutional entrepreneurs facilitate
> institutional changes to develop a DEE?" We find the development of the Danish FinTech DEE
> was enabled and shaped by changes to institutions that support: (i) new collaboration
> dynamics and mechanisms, particularly between incumbent firms and start-ups, (ii)
> adaptations to the regulatory framework, and (iii) the development of global connections.
> Institutional change required collective efforts, depending on different institutional
> entrepreneurs at different times. Neutral leadership of institutional entrepreneurs became
> crucial for institutional change. Furthermore, both system and organisation-level agencies
> influenced institutional change during the development of the DEE.

**Summary (interpretation)**: A unique longitudinal qualitative case study of the **Digital
Entrepreneurial Ecosystem (DEE)** in the Danish FinTech sector — a striking example of a
traditional, highly regulated industry successfully transforming into an emerging technology
sector.

## Research Questions

(i) What institutional forces influence the development of a DEE? (ii) How do **institutional
entrepreneurs** drive institutional change to build a DEE?

## Research Framework

**Digital Entrepreneurial Ecosystem** (DEE, Bejjani et al. 2023) — "complex and dynamic
systems composed of heterogeneous actors that exploit digital technologies for value
co-creation." **Institutional entrepreneurship** (Battilana et al. 2009; Maguire et al. 2004;
DiMaggio 1988) — individual or collective actors who mobilize resources to transform existing
institutions or create new ones. Institutions comprise formal ("hard" — rules and regulations)
and informal (traditions, social conventions) elements (North 1990; Hodgson 2006).
**"Opportunity spaces"** (Grillitsch & Sotarauta 2020) — a conceptual bridge between agency and
structure — 3 dimensions: region-specific, time-specific, agent-specific.

## Data

Case: the Danish FinTech DEE (Copenhagen) — chosen as a rare example of a "peripheral"
financial sector successfully transforming into FinTech. Data: **17 in-depth semi-structured
interviews** (14 participants — FinTech firm founders/leaders, university researchers, a
mentor, an ecosystem representative body, government), field observation (3 days at a FinTech
co-working space plus a Nordic FinTech conference), rich secondary data (reports, FinTech
Forum meeting minutes, blogs, industry publications) spanning 2009–2024.

## Methodology

Single longitudinal qualitative case study (Yin 2018). Analysis: thematic (Braun & Clarke
2006) plus narrative analysis, combining deductive and inductive approaches in 3 steps
(constructing a narrative by developmental phase → identifying "who did what" — institutional
entrepreneurs and strategic actions → analyzing agency interactions among actors).

## Key Findings

**4 phases of DEE development**: Pre-phase (2009–2015, the formation of CFIR — Copenhagen
FinTech Innovation & Research, spurred by the 2008 global financial crisis); Phase 1 —
ecosystem (trans)formation (2015–2017, CFIR transforms into an **Ecosystem Representative
Body — ERB**); Phase 2 — ecosystem development (2017–2022, the ERB + the Danish Financial
Supervisory Authority (FSA) establish the FinTech Forum + the FSA Regulatory Sandbox); Phase 3
— ecosystem legitimacy & growth (2022–present, expanding into "Nordic FinTech Week").

**3 main institutional forces (RQ1)**: (i) **collaboration dynamics/mechanisms** —
particularly between incumbents and start-ups; (ii) the **regulatory environment** — the
Danish FSA established a Division of Fintech, the FinTech Forum, the Regulatory Sandbox; (iii)
**global connections** — international delegations, the "Nordic FinTech" regional movement.

**Mechanisms of institutional change (RQ2) — 6 theoretical propositions**: (1) Institutional
change is a **COLLECTIVE movement** of multiple institutional entrepreneurs over time — NOT the
achievement of a single "hero" (a direct critique of the "heroic view" in earlier literature).
(2) The **neutral leadership** of institutional entrepreneurs is pivotal — the ERB's "neutral"
position helps build legitimacy and bring together actors with differing interests. (3) Both
**system-level agency** AND **organisation-level agency** are SIMULTANEOUSLY necessary —
system-level agency initiates change but organisation-level agency is needed to SUSTAIN it.

## Conclusion

The research contributes to a better understanding of DEE development, and therefore of the
conditions that foster digital entrepreneurship in the FinTech sector — institutional change is
a collective process requiring both system-level and organisation-level action, led by
institutional entrepreneurs with a neutral leadership role.

## Relevance to the Course/Vietnam

- **This is the paper that "opens the black box" of Naudé's framework most concretely across
  all of LN5**: while [[l53-naude-2010-entrepreneurship-development-economics]] (2010) raises
  institutions as a "black box" with an unclear mechanism, Stein et al. (2026) show DIRECTLY
  that institutional entrepreneurs are NOT passive actors under existing institutions but
  actively **change** institutions.
- **Cross-link to [[institutions]] (LN2)**: adds an **endogenous** perspective — rather than
  treating institutions merely as an exogenous variable that determines outcomes (as in the
  AJR framework in [[l21-acemoglu-2001-colonial-origins]]), it shows that actors actively
  create/change institutions — in the same spirit as the Alesina & Giuliano debate
  ([[l24-alesina-giuliano-2015-culture-institutions]], LN2).
- Vietnam connection: the "institutional entrepreneurship" and "neutral leadership" framework
  is useful for analyzing intermediary organizations trying to build a FinTech/digital
  ecosystem in Vietnam (e.g., Napas, FinTech associations, or the State Bank of Vietnam's
  Regulatory Sandbox for FinTech).
