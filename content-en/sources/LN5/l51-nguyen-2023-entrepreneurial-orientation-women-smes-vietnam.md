# L51 — Nguyen (2023), Journal of Asian Finance, Economics and Business 10(1): 223–232

**Author**: Thi Loan Nguyen (Hong Duc University, Thanh Hóa, Vietnam). JEL: B54, B55, D85, M10.

## Summary

Examines how five dimensions of **Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO)** — innovation, competitive
aggressiveness, proactiveness, risk-taking, autonomy — affect the **business performance
(BP)** of women-owned SMEs in Vietnam. A simple random survey of 300 SMEs across several
Vietnamese provinces (sampling frame drawn from the General Statistics Office) yielded 258
valid responses. Result: 4 of the 5 dimensions (innovation, proactiveness, competitive
aggressiveness, autonomy) have a **positive** and significant effect on BP, while
**risk-taking has a NEGATIVE and significant effect** — the opposite of standard EO theory
(which expects risk-taking to be positive; see
[[l53-naude-2010-entrepreneurship-development-economics]] for a similar "productive vs.
unproductive" framing).

## Research Question & Methodology

- **RQ**: how does each dimension of EO affect the BP of women-owned SMEs in Vietnam?
- **EO framework** (Lumpkin & Dess 1996): a multidimensional perspective — the 5 dimensions
  are treated as mutually independent, in contrast with the unidimensional perspective
  (Miller 1983; Wiklund & Shepherd 2005), which treats EO as a single composite trait. The
  paper adopts the multidimensional view in order to disentangle the separate effect of each
  dimension.
- **Five hypotheses** (H1–H5), ALL stated in the **positive** direction: H1 Proactiveness(+),
  H2 Innovation(+), H3 Risk-taking(+), H4 Competitive aggressiveness(+), H5 Autonomy(+).
- **Measurement**: EO — 18 items across 5 factors (scale from Lumpkin & Dess 1996, Alam et al.
  2022), 5-point Likert scale. BP — 6 self-reported items (value added, ROA, ROE, plus overall
  satisfaction, based on the Balanced Scorecard of Kaplan & Norton 1993 and Le et al. 2022).
- **Tests**: Cronbach's α (all >0.7: Inno 0.894, Pro 0.796, Risk 0.848, Compe 0.836,
  Auto 0.915, BP 0.745) → reliable scales. EFA (23 independent variables after dropping 2
  items, Risk1 and Risk4) → 5 factors as expected. Multiple regression (OLS, Enter method) +
  ANOVA; multicollinearity checks (VIF <3), residual autocorrelation (Durbin–Watson),
  homoskedasticity (White test, p>0.05).

## Key Results

- **Model fit**: R² = 0.771, adjusted R² = 0.767 → the 5 EO dimensions explain **76.7%** of
  the variance in BP. ANOVA: F=170.444, Sig.=0.000 → the model fits the empirical data.
  Durbin–Watson = 1.741 (no residual autocorrelation). VIF ranges 1.480–2.500 (<3, no
  multicollinearity).
- **Regression equation** (constant term 0.385, NOT "385" as it appears on the lecture slide —
  this is a typo/missing decimal point on the slide, cross-checked against the original
  Table 5: constant B=0.385, SE=0.202, t=1.907, Sig.=0.058 — the constant term itself is only
  marginally significant at the 10% level, NOT significant at the standard 5% level):

  <span class="stat">BP = 0.385 + 0.328·Inno + 0.301·Pro − 0.111·Risk + 0.134·Compe + 0.220·Auto</span>

- **Individual coefficients** (B, standardized Beta, t, Sig.): Innovation 0.328 (β=0.339,
  t=7.407, p<0.001) — the strongest driver; Proactiveness 0.301 (β=0.246, t=5.260, p<0.001);
  Autonomy 0.220 (β=0.189, t=4.177, p<0.001); Competitive aggressiveness 0.134 (β=0.120,
  t=2.518, p=0.012) — the weakest among the positive group; **Risk-taking −0.111 (β=−0.162,
  t=−4.437, p<0.001)** — clearly negative and statistically significant.
- **A notable point**: the paper concludes that "all 5 hypotheses are accepted" (§4.4.2), but
  this phrasing is MISLEADING — H3 states that risk-taking has a **positive** effect, while
  the empirical result yields a significant **negative** coefficient. In the Discussion
  section, the authors themselves acknowledge this result "contrasts" with Akinyele et al.
  (2021) and Alam et al. (2022) (who find risk-taking to be positive in Nigeria/Malaysia), but
  is consistent with Mandawa (2016, Zambia) and Nuong (2022, Thanh Hóa — the same author as
  this paper). The explanation offered: the women entrepreneurs in the sample tend to rely on
  personal intuition when making business decisions and lack long-term planning, so
  risk-taking in this context is not substantive and sometimes leads to significant failure.
- **Sample profile**: the majority of women SME owners are aged 35–50 (60%), with 3–8 years
  of experience (38.4%) — a relatively late start to entrepreneurship (typically after having
  worked as an employee or cooperative manager before establishing their own SME); 69% of
  firms are micro-enterprises (<50 employees); 65.1% operate in trade and services.

## Significance for the Course/Vietnam

- Vietnam context: SMEs account for ~98% of all enterprises and are the main driver of
  employment and technological growth (Le et al. 2022); women-owned SMEs account for 26.5% of
  currently operating enterprises, tied to the national gender equality goals for 2020–2030
  (VCCI 2021).
- Directly related to [[l52-huis-2019-get-training-microfinance-vietnam]]: both papers study
  women's economic empowerment in Vietnam but differ fundamentally in design — L51 is a
  cross-sectional survey/regression that measures only the **correlation** between EO and BP
  without identifying causality; L52 is an **RCT** (randomized controlled trial) that measures
  the **causal** effect of training on empowerment. Placed side by side, the two papers
  clearly illustrate the methodological distinction (correlational vs. causal) when studying
  the same topic of women's entrepreneurship in Vietnam.
- The negative risk-taking finding is a good exam highlight — it can be linked to the
  "productive vs. unproductive/destructive entrepreneurship" framework (Baumol 1990, cited in
  [[l53-naude-2010-entrepreneurship-development-economics]]): risk-taking does not
  automatically create value; its effectiveness depends on the INSTITUTIONAL ENVIRONMENT and
  the incentive structure that channels that risk.
- Part of [[entrepreneurship-and-development]] (LN5) — a micro/firm-level instantiation of the
  broader agenda set out by
  [[l53-naude-2010-entrepreneurship-development-economics]].
