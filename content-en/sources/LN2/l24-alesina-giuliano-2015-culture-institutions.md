# L24 — Alesina & Giuliano (2015), Journal of Economic Literature 53(4): 898–944

**Authors**: Alberto Alesina (Harvard University, IGIER Bocconi), Paola Giuliano (UCLA
Anderson School of Management). JEL: D02, D72, I32, J12, Z13.

## Abstract

> A growing body of empirical work measuring different types of cultural traits has shown that
> culture matters for a variety of economic outcomes. This paper focuses on one specific
> aspect of the relevance of culture: its relationship to institutions. We review work with a
> theoretical, empirical, and historical bent to assess the presence of a two-way causal effect
> between culture and institutions. (JEL D02, D72, I32, J12, Z13)

**Summary (interpretation)**: A survey focused on ONE specific mechanism in the "culture
matters for development" literature: the relationship between **culture** and **institutions**.
Culture and institutions are **endogenous** variables, possibly jointly determined by
geography, technology, disease, war, and other historical shocks. The paper reviews
theoretical, empirical, and historical evidence to assess whether a **two-way causal effect**
exists between the two.

## Research Questions

Culture and institutions are jointly endogenous, both possibly determined by geography,
technology, disease, war, and other historical shocks. Question: does any causal link exist
between the two variables? How do culture and institutions interact?

## Research Framework

**Definition of culture**: empirically (Guiso, Sapienza & Zingales 2006) = "customary beliefs
and values that ethnic, religious, and social groups transmit fairly unchanged from generation
to generation" (combining values and beliefs). Theoretically: values/beliefs are separate;
per Greif, "cultural beliefs" are a shared idea coordinating interaction, not something
empirically discovered. **Review structure**: Section 2 — defining & measuring
culture/institutions. Section 3 — reviewing the culture↔institutions relationship along 3
directions: (A) culture→institutions, (B) institutions→culture, (C) mutual interaction + joint
determination of development. Section 4 — Conclusions.

**Opening case illustrating the complexity**: Putnam (1993) — the 1970 reform establishing 15
new Italian regional governments (implementing a 1945 constitutional provision, treated as
exogenous to regional development); operation differed markedly North-South. Putnam argues the
"social capital" difference (cooperation, participation, social interaction, trust)
originates from whether the region had been a medieval (12th century) **free city**.

## Key Findings

### (A) Culture → Institutions

- **Litina and other authors**: individualism, measured at the national level, has a
  positive, significant effect on institutions (using settler mortality — Acemoglu, Johnson &
  Robinson 2001 — as an instrument for institutions, testing the reverse causal direction).
  But the institutions→culture effect (reverse direction) is NOT robust across
  specifications.
- **Tabellini (2008a, 2010)**: comparing 8 European countries at the regional level — the
  degree of "generalized morality" differs by history, is persistent, and correlates with
  good current institutions and conditions favorable to development.

### (B)–(C) Institutions ↔ Culture, Jointly Determining Development

- **Michalopoulos & Papaioannou (2013, 2014)** — Africa: precolonial political centralization
  (not national institutions) explains development differences within the same ethnicity;
  colonial borders split over 200 ethnic groups — national institutions do NOT explain
  economic differences within a divided ethnic group — cultural/ethnic traits matter more than
  national institutions in Africa.
- **Engerman & Sokoloff (1997)**: both culture and institutions are **indirect** mechanisms
  through which geography (land suited to large-scale sugar/coffee/rice cultivation) created a
  small elite, spread slavery, and perpetuated unequal institutions.
- **Bisin & Verdier (2015)**: culture and institutions can be **complements or substitutes**
  depending on the economic environment/initial conditions.

Guiso, Sapienza & Zingales (2013) empirically test the Putnam case: modern Italian cities'
social capital correlates with free-city history — one institutional variable (the free-city
arrangement) causes long-run cultural change, and the free city itself originated from an
institutional feature (early local self-governance) → illustrating the culture↔institutions
loop.

## Conclusion

**Central conclusion**: it is wrong to claim causal superiority for ONE of the two variables.
Culture and institutions **interact and co-evolve in a complementary way**, with mutual
feedback effects. The same institutions may function differently under different cultures;
culture also evolves differently depending on institution type. **2 remaining research
tasks**: (1) understanding the interaction mechanism — most research still examines events
separately, while the joint culture-institutions dynamic may be **highly nonlinear**; (2)
understanding the causal channel — most research tries to isolate one causal direction via
IV/historical shocks, but **complementarity between culture and institutions impedes
identification**.

## Relevance to the Course/Vietnam

- The Putnam/free-cities-in-Italy case and the Michalopoulos & Papaioannou Africa case are 2
  powerful illustrations of the limits of a pure-Acemoglu-institutions framework — national
  institutions may matter less than cultural/historical-local traits in some contexts — a good
  counterweight when an essay relies solely on national institutions.
- Directly related to [[l21-acemoglu-2001-colonial-origins]]: this very paper is used as an
  instrument (settler mortality) in a study reviewed here (testing culture→institutions),
  showing the AJR framework is widely reused across the literature beyond its original scope.
- Exam prep question: why does "complementarity" between culture and institutions create
  difficulties for traditional IV identification? Give the Putnam/free-cities example
  illustrating the two-way loop.
