## 3. What Changed From the Previous Prediction (2026-08-04/07)

The previous version (inferring via cluster-trunk/convergence reasoning, without a real
shortlist) predicted 12/20 CORRECTLY and 8/20 INCORRECTLY relative to this new evidence — a clear
demonstration of why indirect inference is far less reliable than a direct precedent:

- **Correct both times (12 papers)**: L11, L13, L21, L31, L42, L52, L61, L64, L71, L86, L95,
  L101.
- **The old version picked WRONG (8 papers, now dropped from the Top 20)**: L81 (Su, Heshmati —
  turns out the professor picked L86 as his own CE representative, not both), L83 (Kirchherr —
  turns out the professor picked L84 García-Barragán for the CE-definition slot), L26 (Huynh &
  Tran — turns out the professor prioritizes the theoretical L24 Alesina & Giuliano over the VN
  application paper), L96 (Kuosmanen/Heshmati LN9 — turns out the professor picked the L93 review
  instead of his own digital-intensity paper), L106 (Dang et al. LN10 — turns out the professor
  picked the L107 review instead of the DiD paper), L22 (Mauro — not picked despite being a
  classic), L53 (Naudé — turns out the professor prioritizes the 2 VN application papers L51/L52
  over the theoretical trunk paper), L77 (Drama — not picked).
- **The old version MISSED (8 papers, now added to the Top 20)**: L24, L32, L45, L51, L73, L84,
  L93, L107.
- **Methodological lesson**: when the professor chooses among several "equivalent" papers within
  the same lecture (e.g. 2 same-author CE papers L81 vs. L86, or a theoretical trunk paper vs. a
  VN application paper), the observed tendency is to prioritize: (a) only 1 paper per author when
  multiple same-author papers exist within one lecture — NOT both; (b) for LN5
  (entrepreneurship), 2 VN APPLICATION papers over the international theoretical trunk; (c) for
  LN2, the opposite — the original THEORY paper over the VN application. No single rule applies
  to every lecture — this is exactly why direct observation (the real K31 shortlist) is far more
  reliable than a general inference rule.
