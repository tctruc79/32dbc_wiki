### Slide 4: Pham, et al. (2020). Flood risk assessment using hybrid AI models integrated with multi-criteria decision analysis in Quang Nam Province, Vietnam. Journal of Hydrology, 592, 125815. - 1/3

- Flood risk assessment is an important task for disaster management activities in flood-prone areas. It is crucial to develop accurate flood risk assessment maps.
- This study proposes a flood risk assessment framework which combines flood susceptibility (tendency to be affected by) assessment and flood consequences in forms of human health and financial impact. It develops a flood risk assessment map using Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) method.
- Two hybrid Artificial Intelligence (AI) models of ABMDT (AdaBoost-DT) and BDT (Bagging-DT) are developed with Decision Table (DT) as a base classifier for creating a flood susceptibility map.
- Use 847 flood locations of major flooding events in the years 2007, 2009 and 2013 in Quang Nam province of Vietnam; and 14 flood influencing factors of topography, geology, hydrology and environment to construct and validate the hybrid AI models.
- Quang Nam province is a key economic region of Central Vietnam, bordered in the North by Danang City, and in the South by Dung Quat Economic Zone. The topography of the province is complex varying from high mountains in the west; midland in the middle; delta and coastal areas in the east; Two river basins of Vu.

### Slide 7: Rakholia, et al. (2022). AI-based air quality PM2.5 forecasting models for developing countries: a case study of HCMC, Vietnam. Urban Climate, 46, 101315. - 1/5

- Clean air is a requirement to sustain human health and the environmental ecosystem. Air pollution is a serious problem in many countries which is impacting health (increase heart, asthma, and lung cancer diseases). Air pollution also creates environmental problems (global warming, acid rain, and the ozone layer's depletion).
- Particulate Matter (PM2.5) is considered a hazardous air pollutant to human health. Accurate hourly forecasting of PM2.5 concentrations is important for public health, helping the citizens to alleviate the harmful effects of air pollution on health.
- This study analyses the temporal characteristics of PM2.5 at different locations in Ho Chi Minh City. HCMC is an economic center with 8.99 million population.
- Develop several AI-based PM2.5 forecasting models, with both an hourly forecast granularity (1 h to 24 h) and a 24-h rolling mean. These Machine Learning algorithms include Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) Regressor, hybrid 1D CNN-LSTM, extreme Gradient Boosting Regressor, and Prophet.
- Collected the data from six monitoring stations installed by the Healthy Air project partners at different locations in HCMC, including traffic, residential and industrial areas in the city. Develop a suitable model training protocol using data from a short period to address the non-stationarity of PM2.5 time series. The proposed PM2.5 forecasting models will warn HCMC citizens of air pollution in the city.

### Slide 17: VietDuc, et al. (2024). Measuring the digital economy in Vietnam. Telecommunication Policy, 48, 102683. - 1/4

- First coined in the 1990s, the concept of the digital economy has evolved, reflecting the rapidly changing nature of ICT and its usage by businesses, government and people. In the late 1990s, the analysis was mainly concerned with Internet adoption. In past few years, the focus is on how digital technologies, services, products, techniques, and skills pervade economies. Recent studies have focused on "digitization" and "digital transformation," i.e., how digital products and services spill over and disrupt traditional sectors.
- Use the concept of digital spillover and a panel model to measure the GDP contribution of Vietnam's core digital economy.
- The calculations and analysis give three main results:
- the scale of Vietnam's core economy has increased rapidly over the past 14 years. In 2007, the share of the core economy in GDP was only 1.45%, in 2019 7.08%.
- the scale of Vietnam's digitalized economy grew significantly from 2007 to 2019. In 2007-2011, it accounted for 4.90% of GDP, it increased to 11.56% in 2016-2019.
- growth of the digitalized economy is mainly based on the growth of the core digital economy. The digital spillover effect during the 13 years has only slightly changed.
- This study implies that Vietnam needs policies to develop the core digital economy and especially promote digital transformation to achieve the national digitalized economy goal of 20% of GDP by 2025 and 30% by 2030.

### Slide 18: VietDuc, et al. (2024). Measuring the digital economy in Vietnam. Telecommunication Policy, 48, 102683. - 2/4

- Measuring the digital economy: The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) measures the digital economy through:
- IDI- Information and Communication Technology Development Index (IDI). IDI is divided into three sub-metrics: Access, Use, and Skills. Each sub-index aggregates different aspects and components of the ICT development process.
- The World Economic Forum's NRI- Technology Readiness Index measures a country's ability to take advantage of economic opportunities provided by ICT. NRI 2020 consists of 4 groups of components: (1) Technological readiness, (2) Human readiness, (3) Government readiness, and (4) Impact of ICT on the economy.
- The OECD has developed the Digital Economy and Society Index (DESI) to measure the information society. DESI measures five aspects: (1) Connectivity, (2) Human resources, (3) Internet service usage, (4) Digital transformation of businesses, and (5) Public service.
- Recently, G20 has published a toolkit (G20 DETF) to measure the digital economy with 35 indicators divided into four groups: (1) Infrastructure, (2) Application of innovation and technology, (3) Society, and (4) Employment and growth.
- The statistical method, using the existing national account statistics (NAS), with an improved definition of the ICT sub-sector over time, has the advantage of providing data on the size and percentage of the digital economy in the total GDP.

### Slide 21: Tam, et al. (2024). Investigating potential barriers to construction digitalization in emerging economies: a study in Vietnam. International Journal of Information Management Data Insights, 4(1), 100226. - 1/4

- The construction industry plays a crucial role in the development of any country, contributing to national economies, employment, and infrastructure. Digitalization boost efficiency and quality in construction, but emerging economies face challenges in adopting these technologies.
- Investigates the potential barriers hindering construction digitalization in Vietnam, a rapidly growing market. Surveyed 248 construction professionals across client, contractor, and consultant groups, examine their perceptions of 31 potential barriers.
- Employ statistical techniques such as reliability tests, descriptive statistics, exploratory factor analysis (EFA), and analysis of variance (ANOVA), and uncovered significant differences in barrier importance among the stakeholder groups.
- The findings revealed that social and habitual resistance to change emerged as the most significant barrier to construction digitalization in Vietnam, followed by high costs of software and hardware, lack of market data for technology integration, security concerns, and the absence of standardized practices and guidelines.
- Expanding knowledge on digitalization barriers in emerging economies, empowers policymakers, industry leaders, and researchers with insights for crafting effective strategies to boost construction performance.

- Using a dataset 1993-2023 in China, it employs a combination of social network analysis and deep learning techniques to investigate the impact of digital transformation on high-quality economic development, measured as green TFP.

- Introduces a novel methodological framework integrating data-driven forecasting with systemic policy interventions. Identify critical influencing factors and employing deep learning techniques to predict GTFP, this research broadens interdisciplinary approaches to sustainability.
