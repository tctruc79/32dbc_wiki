### Robinson and Thierfelder (2024). US International Trade Policy: Scenarios of Protectionism and Trade Wars. Journal of Policy Modeling, 46(4), 723-739. 1(3)

- US international trade policy under both the Trump 1.0 and Biden administrations has been increasingly protectionist. The protectionist policy under Trump 2.0 is further intensified.
- This paper considers two policy scenarios: (1) an across-the-board increase in all US tariffs by 10%, and (2) a severe escalation of the US trade war with China. Both cases are already in progress.
- The scenarios are analysed using a multi-country computable general equilibrium (CGE) simulation model of the global trade. Trade wars or protection policy regimes will increase tariffs in many sectors simultaneously and include both final goods and intermediate inputs. The impacts are complex, with both direct and indirect forces impacting both domestic and international markets.
- The CGE model captures these mechanisms, including both short- and long-run effects, with and without retaliation by trade partners. In a world economy where the US accounts for only 10% of global trade and potential rival trade blocs in Europe and East and South-East Asia, the US is no longer hegemonic in global markets. Find that tariffs do not protect US manufacturing jobs. The cost of imported intermediate goods increases, raising manufacturing production cost.
- US trade war with China leads to a dramatic fall in bilateral trade. Other countries expand their bilateral trade with China. Find that the world economy can adjust to US trade wars, diverting trade around the US.

### Robinson and Thierfelder (2024). US International Trade Policy: Scenarios of Protectionism and Trade Wars. Journal of Policy Modeling, 46(4), 723-739. 2(3)

- In the early post-WW2 period, the US dominated the global economy in terms of both GDP and international trade. With European recovery and the evolution of the European Union (EU), Europe gradually formed a highly integrated regional economy that now equals the US in size (GDP) and has a much larger role in global trade.
- In the latter quarter of the 20th century, especially with the emergence of China in the world economy, a similar regional economy evolved in East and Southeast Asia.
- The empirical analysis of trade policy scenarios is done with a multi-country computable general equilibrium (CGE) simulation model of the global economy. The model includes 18 countries/regions, 24 sectors/commodities, two labour categories (skilled and unskilled), and 3 other factors of production (land, capital, and natural resources). The underlying approach to multi-country modelling is the construction of a series of single-country CGE models.

### Robinson and Thierfelder (2024). US International Trade Policy: Scenarios of Protectionism and Trade Wars. Journal of Policy Modeling, 46(4), 723-739. 3(3)

- Scenario analysis: Use the model to analyse the impact of two US trade policy scenarios: (i) uniform increase in 10% tariffs and (ii) an expanded trade war with China. Use the model to do scenario analysis: "what if" simulations of the impact on the global economy of different trade policy regimes.
- The scenarios are not forecasts, but projections of alternative futures under different assumptions about US policy behaviour and feedback reactions by other countries.
- Conclusion: Empirical analysis of two potential scenarios of increased US protectionism (uniform increase in tariffs on all imports and an expanded trade war with China) indicate that, as economic theory and historical experience suggest, protectionist trade policies generate serious costs and limited benefits to the initiating country, US.
- While there are potential gains from exploiting market power on world markets through increasing tariffs, the increased cost of intermediate inputs and inevitable retaliation by trade partners erodes or reverses the potential gains.

### Alessandria, Yor Khan, Khederlarian, Ruhl and Steinberg (2025). Trade War and Peace: US-China Trade and Tariff Risk from 2025-2050. Journal of International Economics, 155, 104066. 1(3)

- Model trade policy use a dynamic exporting model to estimate how expectations about US tariffs on China have changed around the trade war. They find (i) no increase in the likelihood of a trade war before 2018; (ii) the trade war was initially expected to end quickly but its expected duration grew after 2020; and (iii) the trade war reduced the likelihood that China would face Non-Normal Trade Relations tariffs in the future. The findings imply the expected mean future US tariff on China rose more under Biden than under Trump 1.0.
- Donald Trump's election as US President in 2016 raised questions about the future of US trade-policy. Would he follow through on his campaign pledge to raise tariffs on China? If so, by how much? Would he shift China to the Non-Normal Trade Relations (NNTR) tariff schedule or choose something else? How long would these tariffs last? Would he reverse course quickly? or would the tariffs remain in place for decades?
- Once President Trump raised tariffs on China in 2018, the question of how long these tariffs would last was further complicated by the upcoming 2020 election and the subsequent Presidency of Biden. It remained an issue in 2024; in May, Biden renewed the tariffs and increased tariffs by 25% on almost 400 goods.

### Alessandria, Yor Khan, Khederlarian, Ruhl and Steinberg (2025). Trade War and Peace: US-China Trade and Tariff Risk from 2025-2050. Journal of International Economics, 155, 104066. 2(3)

- Model: Use disaggregated U.S. import data and a dynamic trade model with two key features: heterogeneous firms that make forward-looking export participation decisions, and tariff risk varies across products and time. In the model, Chinese firms make investments in US market access subject to idiosyncratic shocks, industry-specific variation in tariffs across policy regimes, and a common time-varying probability of switching between regimes.
- Three main findings are: First, despite Trump's campaign rhetoric, there was no increase in the probability that U.S. tariffs on China would rise before the trade war began. The key data moment that identifies this probability is the trade-war gap elasticity: the elasticity of U.S. imports from China to the gap between the trade-war tariffs and Normal Trade Relations (NTR) tariffs. This elasticity was stable in the three years before the Trump tariffs were put in place.
- Second, during the first two years of the trade war, the probability that tariffs would return to NTR levels was very high-more than 70%. Expectations about the end of the trade war began to shift when Biden continued the trade war. By 2023, the probability of the trade war ending had fallen to 21%.
- Third, the trade war shifted the nature of the uncertainty about US trade policy towards China. Prior to the trade war, there existed a possibility of reverting to NNTR tariffs. This possibility still existed after China was granted Permanent NTR in 2001, but it fell when the trade war began.

### Alessandria, Yor Khan, Khederlarian, Ruhl and Steinberg (2025). Trade War and Peace: US-China Trade and Tariff Risk from 2025-2050. Journal of International Economics, 155, 104066. 3(3)

- Empirical analysis: Use US import data from the US Census Bureau (July 2014-June 2024) and Eurostat import data for the 27 EU countries. Aggregate the EU countries into a single importer. For the US and the EU, China is treated as a separate exporter, while all other exporters are aggregated into a second group.
- Structural model: The empirical findings are inputs to the structural model used to measure the dynamics of expectations about US trade policy towards China and distinguish the trade effects from the gradual adjustment to the trade-war tariffs. The dynamic exporter model is introducing a richer stochastic process for trade policy featuring multidimensional tariff risk.
- Results: First, discuss the model's ability to account for the trade dynamics around the trade war and the path of trade-policy expectations implied by these dynamics. Second, study the implications of the estimates for the future of US-China trade. Finally, relate the current substitution and risks to the trade liberalization in 1980.
- Conclusion: The trade war between the US and China that began in 2018 demonstrated that China's Permanent Normal Trade Relations status did not eliminate trade-policy risk, and that the nature of this risk had changed. At the beginning of the trade war, the expected path of future tariffs fell because the trade-war tariffs were expected to be quickly reversed, and the likelihood of NNTR had diminished. As the trade war continued, expected tariffs grew.

### Che, Lin and Zhang (2025). Pains or Gains: Trade War, Trade Deficit, and Tariff Evasion. Journal of International Economics, 155, 104090. 1(3)

- This paper reveals that the reduction in the US-China trade deficit during the trade war obscured reporting discrepancies in US imports of Chinese products due to tariff evasion. Empirically examine the effect of the US-China trade war on tariff evasion in US imports of Chinese goods and provide direct evidence that market demand of entry states contributes to tariff evasion.
- Using the input-output table, find that a one standard deviation increase in local demand causes a 1.3-fold rise in tariff evasion for affected products post-trade war. This effect mainly works through intermediate goods, and its impact grows as importers' tariff liabilities increase. The voters' attitudes toward trade protection and the development of labour unions play crucial roles in mediating the influence of market demand on tariff evasion.
- The papers contribution. First, it contributes to the debate on the effectiveness of the US-China trade war by pointing out the differences in reported trade deficits. Second, at the micro level, previous studies have focused on how US importers responded to US-China trade war through trade diversion and global value chains. Third, existing studies on the causes of tariff evasion focus on customs, attributing tariff evasion to the product inspection, inefficiency of and corruption within customs administrations.
- Fourth, the paper contributes to the literature on the downstream effects of trade barriers. In exploring the impacts of changes in tariffs, studies are not confined to the direct effects but also look at the spillover effects of tariffs through input-output linkages in downstream industries.

### Che, Lin and Zhang (2025). Pains or Gains: Trade War, Trade Deficit, and Tariff Evasion. Journal of International Economics, 155, 104090. 3(3)

- Trade war and tariff evasion: Presents the effects of the trade war on promoting tariff evasion behaviour during the importation process of Chinese products into the US. The results indicate that compared with EU imports, the trade war prompted US importers to evade the tariffs through methods such as underreporting unit values and quantities and misclassifying product categories. By 2021, the gap value caused by these mechanisms explained 69.70% of the trade gaps.
- Heterogeneity analysis: The preceding analysis showed a significant increase in evasion of tariffs on Chinese imports following the trade war. Moreover, it highlighted the heterogeneous impacts of local demand intensity on tariff evasion. The local market conditions in the US directly influenced tariff evasion behaviour induced by the trade war. The political and social environments in various regions may have affected the result.
- Conclusion: This paper has shown that the US-China trade war was not as successful in reducing the trade deficit between China and the US as Trump has described, mainly due to importers evading paying customs tariffs. The paper contributes to the literature by describing a more realistic image of the effects of the trade war on the "trade deficit," which is of great significance for trade policy formation. Currently, tariff evasion is generally attributed to the reporting value due to importers.

### Sheng, L., Song, H. and Zheng, X. (2025). How did Chinese Exporters Manage the trade War? Journal of International Money and Finance, 153, 103300. 1(5)

- In 2018, Trump initiated a trade war with China, marking a departure from trade liberalization. As tariffs between the two economies escalated, and research into impacts of the trade war on various fronts, including bilateral trade, welfare, tariff pass-through, output, and consumption expanded.
- The strategic navigation of trade diversion by Chinese exporters remained understudied. Did China adeptly reallocate its exports to alternative markets? If so, were Chinese products diverted more to the North or the South? How did the trade diversion affect China's terms of trade in third markets? Addressing those questions contributes to academic discourse and offers insights for policymakers and businesses navigating in times heightened protectionism.
- This paper studies how Chinese exporters managed the recent US tariff hikes. Contrary to the conventional wisdom of horizontal trade diversion, China did not divert more of its products to other Northern countries but more to the South.
- Moving down the quality ladder of destinations helps Chinese exporters escape competition for high-quality products in the North and lowers penetration costs in the South.
- This vertical trade diversion reduces quality-adjusted export prices but raises qualities and gross prices of Chinese diverted exports, particularly in poor countries and for products with high quality scopes, implying that it may benefit the South more.

### Sheng, L., Song, H. and Zheng, X. (2025). How did Chinese Exporters Manage the trade War? Journal of International Money and Finance, 153, 103300. 2(5)

- Trade diversion: examine Chinese exporters' trade diversion strategy. Summarize the two trade diversion strategies as two hypotheses.
- Hypothesis 1. Horizontal Trade Diversion (HTD) refers to the strategy where Chinese exporters redirect their products to countries that are similar to the US in terms of income level, market access, and consumer preferences. The HTD hypothesis posits that, in response to the US tariff hikes, Chinese exporters will divert their exports to other high-income Northern countries with similar market characteristics to the US. These markets would have similar demand and economic conditions, making them natural alternatives for trade diversion.
- Hypothesis 2. Vertical Trade Diversion (VTD) involves redirecting exports down the quality ladder, meaning high-quality products are diverted to lower-income countries (the South) that typically import lower-quality products from China. The VTD hypothesis posits that, in response to the US tariff hikes, Chinese exporters will primarily adopt a VTD strategy, diverting high-quality products to lower-income Southern countries.

### Almazan-Gomez, El Khatabi, Llano and Perez (2025). Modelling Regional Exposure to New Trade Wars. Journal of Policy Modeling, 47. 1(3)

- The COVID-19 pandemic and recent geopolitical events have triggered a new wave of trade protectionism. With the return of Trump, a new trade war has just started. By addressing the territorial effect of trade policy, this study quantifies the effects of two protectionism measures announced by the US against the EU before the pandemic: the Boeing-Airbus trade dispute and a 25% tariff on the automotive sector. The impacts are calculated for 297 NUTS-2 European regions combining partial equilibrium and multiregional input-output analysis.
- The results reveal that the new tariffs would negatively impact the European economy, with unevenly distributed effects across regions. The most affected regions are those heavily specialized in targeted products, such as automotive and aerospace components, and those deeply integrated into European value chains, experiencing indirect impacts.
- There is need for targeted policy interventions at the European, national, and regional levels to mitigate the adverse effects.
- Key policy recommendations include developing contingency plans, promoting sectoral and geographical diversification, and strengthening intra-European trade relations. The findings highlight the strategic importance of the EU's engagement in global trade negotiations to protect its economic interests and support vulnerable regions. This study wants also to illustrate the potential effects of the new tariffs against the EU to be applied in the first year of Trumps second term.

### Almazan-Gomez, El Khatabi, Llano and Perez (2025). Modelling Regional Exposure to New Trade Wars. Journal of Policy Modeling, 47. 2(3)

- Concluding remarks: This work provides a detailed analysis of the territorial impacts of protectionist trade policies at the NUTS-2 level in Europe, using an approach that combines a partial equilibrium model (SMART) with an interregional input-output (IO) framework. The results highlight the significant and varied effects of new tariffs on EU, underscoring the importance of considering both direct and indirect impacts in trade policy.
- The empirical results demonstrate that protectionist measures, such as those announced by the US against the EU, can generate substantial economic shocks directly or indirectly linked to the targeted sectors. The most vulnerable regions are those with a high degree of specialization and strong export linkages with the US, as well as those embedded in European value chains. These findings highlight the deeply interconnected nature of modern economies and the potential for widespread disruptions triggered by unilateral trade policies.
- The methodological framework developed provides policymakers with a valuable tool to assess the regional and sectoral exposure to trade restrictions. In analysis of the economic repercussions of tariffs, this model supports the design of targeted mitigation strategies and economic resilience. The relevance of this approach is further underscored when considering policies that may be perceived as protectionist by trading partners, such as the Common Agricultural Policy, sanctions against Russia following the invasion of Ukraine, or the European Border Carbon Tax Mechanism.

### Dang, Yawei, and Abdullah (2024). The impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnamese export to the US: a quantitative study using DiD approach. Journal of Trade Science, 12(4), 304-318. 1(3)

- Background: China has a large surplus in trade with US. China has become a threat to US interest in Asia-Pacific region and globally. US has started a trade war against what they call unfair trade practices.
- Purpose: The study examines the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnamese exports to the US. US has consistently served as a key market for Vietnamese goods and services in recent decades. The heterogeneous effects of the trade war on different export sectors are also evaluated.
- Methodology: The secondary data on Vietnamese exports to the US at a 6-digit level is collected from UN Comtrade. The difference-in-differences (DiD) method is employed to analyse the impact of the trade war on exports from Vietnam to the US.
- Findings: The results revealed a 14% increase in total Vietnamese exports to the US due to the trade war. Examining heterogeneous trade war effects, plastics, iron or steel articles, textiles and garments, and machinery and mechanical appliances, experienced significant benefits. However, no effects on other sectors, such as electrical machinery products, agricultural and forestry, and furniture was found.
- Contribution: The paper is one among limited studies considering the causal effects of the trade war on a developing country, accounting for the heterogeneous effects on different export sectors.

### Dang, Yawei, and Abdullah (2024). The impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnamese export to the US: a quantitative study using DiD approach. Journal of Trade Science, 12(4), 304-318. 2(3)

- Discussion and implications: The finding of a positive effect on total exports is in line with predictions of trade diversion theory, which suggests that the US and China should reduce their bilateral trade while increase their exports from bystander countries. The result is also consistent with findings from previous studies, which found positive effects of the trade war on third nations, including Vietnam.
- Regarding the heterogeneous effects of the trade war, industries such as iron or steel articles, machinery and mechanical appliances, and textile and garments benefit from the trade war, mainly because the prices of Vietnamese goods have become relatively cheaper compared to Chinese goods after China faced an increase in import tariffs from the US.
- Did not find significant impacts of the trade war on the other consumption goods such as smartphones, wood products, furniture, agriculture, forestry and aquatic products. It may come from the fact that those consumption goods are not targeted by the US.
- The results provide some suggestions for policymakers and exporting firms when exporting their commodities to the US - one of the most important traders with Vietnam.

### Dang, Yawei, and Abdullah (2024). The impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnamese export to the US: a quantitative study using DiD approach. Journal of Trade Science, 12(4), 304-318. 3(3)

- Conclusion: The US-China trade war has had huge impacts, not only on China and the US but also, on other countries. Vietnam is significantly affected by the trade war. The study is quantifying the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's exports to the US using the DiD approach and consider the heterogeneous effects on different export sectors.
- The results revealed that the US-China trade war has led to an approximate 14% rise in Vietnam's total exports to the US. Certain sectors (textiles and garments, machinery and mechanical appliances, plastics, and iron or steel products) have experienced significant positive impacts. The increased US tariffs on Chinese imports have boosted the competitiveness of Vietnamese products.
- Did not find evidence of the impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's exports of agricultural products, machinery and electronic equipment, and furniture to the US. The consumption commodities are not a targeted group in the US tariff lists. To enhance exports to the US, Vietnam should maintain its competitive advantage, improve the quality of export products, effectively implement origin tracing and attract good foreign investors to set up their business in the country.

### Toai (2025). Impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's import and export. Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 10(46s), 907-914. 1(3)

- Background: The US-China trade war, initiated in 2018, has significantly reshaped global trade flows, influencing the economic trajectories of several countries, including Vietnam.
- Purpose: This paper explores the impact of the trade tensions between the world's two largest economies on Vietnam's import and export structure.
- Methodology: Using secondary data and recent literature, examine the trade war's implications for Vietnam's export growth to the US, shifts in its import patterns from China and changes in global value chains.
- Finding: Finds that Vietnam has benefited from trade diversion effects, enhancing its position as a manufacturing and export hub. However, it also faces challenges such as increasing dependence on imported inputs, risks of protectionist measures, and heightened competition from regional peers.
- Conclusion: Concludes with policy recommendations for Vietnam to sustain its trade performance amid ongoing geopolitical tensions.

### Toai (2025). Impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's import and export. Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 10(46s), 907-914. 2(3)

- Discussion: The findings reveal a portrait of Vietnam as both a strategic beneficiary of and a vulnerable participant in the trade war. While Vietnam has seized opportunities for export growth and FDI, the sustainability of these trade diversion gains remains uncertain due to structural dependencies on Chinese inputs - vertical specialization (add labour to assemble and re-export final products) and geopolitical risks.
- It has triggered strategic concern within U.S. policy circles, with accusations of currency manipulation, transshipment, and unfair trade practices. This study reveals a structural imbalance: Vietnam exports nearly five times more than it imports. This asymmetric trade structure is not sustainable in the long run and exposes Vietnam to retaliatory trade measures, including anti-dumping and potential loss of trade privileges.
- The findings also point to missed strategic opportunities. Despite increased exports, Vietnam has not substantially improved its value-added position in the global supply chain. Much of the country's export volume consists of assembled products rather than designed or branded goods. The lack of diversification in both input sources (from China) and export destinations (US) suggests vulnerability to external shocks.

### Toai (2025). Impact of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's import and export. Journal of Information Systems Engineering and Management, 10(46s), 907-914. 3(3)

- Conclusion: Examined the complex effects of the US-China trade war on Vietnam's import and export structure, 2020-2024. Vietnam has emerged as a prominent short-term beneficiary of the trade war through increased exports to the US, FDI, and strategic vulnerabilities demanding proactive policy responses.
- Vietnam has captured diverted trade flows from China, in electronics, textiles, and furniture. Exports to the US surged by over 40% from 2020 to 2024, making the U.S. Vietnam's largest export market. The export-led growth has relied heavily on imported intermediate goods from China. The manufacturing is exposed to supply chain disruptions, price volatility, and political tension.
- Vietnam's trade surplus with the US more than doubled over five years has attracted regulatory scrutiny and geopolitical attention. It can result in retaliatory measures like anti-dumping duties, loss of trade privileges, or currency pressure. These imbalances can undermine the long-term stability of bilateral relations.
- Vietnam's success should rest on structural improvements. Policymakers must focus on diversifying input sources, strengthening domestic supply chains, enhancing value-added production, and recalibrating trade diplomacy to maintain a better balance.
- In conclusion, the US-China trade war has presented Vietnam with window of opportunity and a mirror of structural limitations. Whether Vietnam remains a tactical substitute in global supply chains or evolves into a strategic hub of high-value production will depend on its ability to act upon the economic lessons.

- The transition revitalized the economy, laying the groundwork for additional market-oriented initiatives in the late 1980s, and ultimately accelerating Vietnam's integration into the global economy. - The interplay between trade liberalization, poverty, and income inequality remains a critical area. Comparative advantage and specialization leads to mutual gains from trade, potentially boosting overall welfare. Theoretically, this dynamic can alleviate poverty by creating formal employment opportunities.

### Ngo-Lee (2025), Income inequality, poverty and trade liberalization: Evidence from the Vietnam-US bilateral trade agreement. Research in Economics. 4(4)

- Investigates how tariff reductions, introduced by the Vietnam-US Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA), shaped patterns of income inequality and poverty in Vietnam from 2002 to 2018. Using a province-level dataset derived from the VHLS Survey and tariff measures, the analysis focuses on the P80/P20 ratio for income inequality and the poverty headcount ratio as key outcomes.
- The findings indicate that while overall poverty rates declined following the BTA, in more industrialized regions such as the Red River Delta and the Southeast, the effects on income inequality were not uniform. In certain areas, notably the Mekong River Delta, reliance on agriculture and limited diversification may have left households vulnerable to global price fluctuations, offsetting the potential gains from enhanced market access.
- Conversely, regions with robust infrastructure, diverse industrial bases, and higher FDI witnessed a more pronounced drop in both income inequality and poverty. These results underscore the importance of regional characteristics, including sector composition and connectivity, in determining the distributional consequences of trade liberalization.
