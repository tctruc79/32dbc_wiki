### Baron et al. (2021) Banking crisis without panics. QJE. 1(21)

- The 2008 global financial crisis renewed interest in understanding the causes and consequences of banking crisis. Banking crisis is an episode where the banking sector's ability to intermediate funds is impaired, because equity holders are first to suffer losses. Panics is an episode of severe and sudden withdrawal of funding by bank creditors.
- Baron et al. examine historical banking crises through the lens of bank equity (assets) declines. It cover a broad sample of episodes of banking distress with and without banking panics. Construct a new data set on bank equity returns on banking panics for 46 countries, 1870-2016.
- They find that even in the absence of panics, large bank equity declines are associated with substantial credit contractions and output gaps. Results show that panics are not necessary for banking crises to have severe economic consequences. Panics tend to be preceded by large bank equity declines, suggesting that panics are the result, rather than the cause of earlier bank losses. Create a historical banking crisis chronology that distinguishes between bank equity losses and panics.

### Introduction 2(21)

- Three research questions: (i) Are large bank equity declines associated with adverse macroeconomic consequences?, (ii) Are panics necessary for banking crises to have severe economic consequences?. (iii) If panics occur, do they tend to precipitate the crisis or occur after large declines in bank equity?
- To capture episodes of bank distress, define a "bank equity crash" as an annual bank equity decline of over 30%. Use monthly data covering over 100 banking crises, find that large bank equity declines tend to precede panics and credit spread spikes.
- The findings paint a more complete picture of the roles played by bank equity declines and panics during banking crises. Large bank equity declines tend to be followed by severe economic consequences even without panics. Large bank equity declines precede panics; and panics with large bank equity declines tend to have the most severe credit contractions and output gaps.
- Panics is an amplification mechanism, but not a necessary condition for severe banking crises. The findings reinforce the importance of timely recapitalization of bank capital during early phases of banking distress, rather than having policymakers simply backstop liquidity, to prevent subsequent panics from erupting and to minimize adverse macroeconomic consequences.

### Bank equity crashes and credit spread spikes 18(21)

- What is the relationship between bank equity declines and credit spread increases? Policy makers tend to use disruptions in credit markets as indicators of panics by bank creditors. Credit spread spikes serve as our proxy of disruptions in credit markets.

- Taken together, the analysis in this subsection shows that bank equity crashes tend to precede credit spread spikes, which motivates policy makers to pay more attention to bank equity declines in assessing the risk of an emerging banking crisis.

### Bank and nonfinancial equity crashes 19(21)

- Figure VI also showed that bank equity tends to peak and decline earlier than nonfinancial equity during banking crises. Table III, Panel B, column (1) confirms this result by showing that bank equity crashes precede nonfinancial equity crashes by average of 1.94 months.

### Conclusion 21(21)

- Baron et al. (2021) construct a new historical data set of bank equity returns for 46 countries going back to 1870. Document that large bank equity declines are a strong predictor of lower GDP growth and bank credit-to-GDP.
- The relation between bank equity returns and subsequent macroeconomic outcomes is highly nonlinear. Bank equity is informative about severe negative macroeconomic events involving a decline in intermediated credit.
- To separate crises, including banking crises with and without panics. Find that non-panic banking distress also translate into output gaps and credit contractions. Panics, come after substantial bank equity declines.
- The results suggest that the defining feature of a banking crisis is a bank capital crunch. These capital crunches lead bank creditors to run on bank debt, especially once large losses have been realized, and banks are undercapitalized.
- When panics are averted by guarantees, an undercapitalized banking system is still unable to service the economy. Regulators focus on bank capital adequacy during emerging crises. Bank equity and credit expansion measures, provide useful barometers of the health of the banking sector.
- The results provide new insights into the roles of bank losses and panics, but they cannot causally identify the role of bank losses and panics in depressing bank lending and output.

### Jiang, L., Levine, R., & Lin, C. (2019). Competition and bank liquidity creation. JFQA. 1(11)

- Liquidity creation is a vital banking service. Banks create liquidity by using relatively liquid liabilities, such as demand deposits, to fund relatively illiquid assets, such as business loans.
- Banks also create liquidity off the balance sheet by providing loan commitments and standby letters of credit that allow firms to develop and modify long-run investment strategies efficiently.
- By creating liquidity, banks enable investments in positive net present value projects that would be infeasible without bank liquidity creation.
- Jian et al. (2019) use a new identification strategy to assess whether an intensification of competition among banks increases or decreases the provision of banks liquidity creation. The theory offers conflicting predictions about the impact of competition on liquidity creation.
- They find that regulatory-induced competition reduces liquidity creation. Consistent with models suggesting that banks pushed toward insolvency reduce risk-taking activities. Regulatory-induced competition reduces liquidity creation more among less profitable banks with less risk-absorbing capacity.

### Empirical Research on Liquidity Creation 2(11)

- Given the importance of liquidity creation, a body of empirical research examines its determinants. Berger and Bouwman (2009) created measures of liquidity creation for U.S. banks from 1993 to 2003. They evaluate the connections between bank capital, bank size, and liquidity creation. The Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) increased bank lending, a key component of liquidity creation. Berger and Bouwman (2016) provide a review of the liquidity creation literature.
- Jiang et al. (2019) provide empirical evaluation of whether U.S. bank regulatory reforms that lowered barriers to competition among banks it increased, decreased, or had no effect on liquidity creation. Theory offers two perspectives:
- Competition reduces liquidity creation by lowering the risk absorption capacity, and
- Suggests mechanisms through which competition boosts liquidity creation.
- The major challenge to assessing the impact of competition on liquidity creation is identification. There are two deficiencies, with using the first year of removal of barriers to interstate banking impacting on liquidity creation:
- Other developments might have occurred at the same time the state deregulated interstate banking that might shape bank liquidity creation. See Figure 1.

### Conclusion 11(11)

### Conclusions 7(7)

- This paper described a new methodology to nowcast the economic effects of the COVID-19 crisis. The method can also be used to predict recovery for economic activities in small, export-oriented countries, both across industries and for aggregate economies.
- The technique relies on two sets of available data: observed trend deviations in industry-level GDP, and real-time variation in sectoral financial indexes. The financial indexes are used to update industry-level trend deviations of output and extend them into the future.
- The application to Vietnam shows that the method can match more traditional techniques used by government agencies and international organizations; the nowcasted recovery for Vietnam from fall 2020 are more in line with newest data than those made the IMF.

### Cai and Le (2023). Natural resources and financial development: Role of CSR on green economic growth in Vietnam. RP 1(4)

- Pollution is a threat to all ecosystems. As developing economies grow and industrialize, they increase the use of fossil fuels. It results in rapid depletion of resources and increase in waste, residues, and GHGs.
- The relationship among natural resources, financial development, and the role of corporate social responsibility (CSR) on green economic growth in Vietnam is analyzed. Apply the Pooled Mean Group-Autoregressive Distributed Lag (PMG-ARDL) model to assess this relationship for 1990-2018.
- The variables are cointegrated. The CSR, financial development, and natural resources development all have a long-term positive association but a short-term negative relationship with green economic growth.
- If efficient fiscal and financial management measures are not implemented, the panel nations' public debt sustainability will be jeopardized due to CSR's overreliance on natural resources rents (NRR).
- Natural resources may have a detrimental impact on financial growth if laws are not implemented. CSR regulations in Vietnam may reduce greenhouse gases (GHG) emissions and promote green economic growth. If this criterion is met, NRR-related improvements in financial development may be sustained.

### Data and method 2(4)

- Data: The WDI of the CSR dataset is used for 1990-2018, available data for Vietnam (1999-2018). Borrowing is government debt as a proportion of GDP.
- Methodology steps: (1) to assess stationarity of the variables using unit root, (2) the Johansen-Fisher and Kao tests are used to demonstrate a long-term connection between the variables, (3) the PMG model calculated long- and short-term outcomes, (4) vector error correction model (VECM) was used to establish the link between overall natural capital revenues and debt levels, (5) Wald test is used establish brief causation, and (6) the line of causation among overall resource revenues and debt levels is defined using bilateral causation.
- Variables: The CSR is overall natural capital prices (the sum of rents from petroleum, gasoline, lignite, minerals, and forestry), represented as a percentage of GDP. lnEFP, lnGDP (per capita), lnNRR, lnMER, lnNAT, lnURB are explanatory variables.
- Dynamic panel estimation techniques, ARDL approach are used to investigate the short-and long-term dynamic links.
- In CSR, PMG and MG are used to estimate the coefficient of N time series regression models, are averaged to get the PMG estimate. PMG estimate is preferred over the MG if the p-value is greater than 0.05.

### Conclusion and Recommendations 4(4)

- Analyzed the effect of financial development, natural resources utilization, and CSR on green economic growth in Vietnam. A yearly balanced panel data for Vietnam from 1999 to 2018 is used to support empirical findings. Using the PMG-ARDL technique accounting for areas heterogeneity.
- The empirical findings are summarized using the CSR index, an indicator of environmental efficiency to promote green economic growth. The importance of CSR and natural resources as mediating factors in the connection between green economic growth and sustainable performance is highlighted.
- The findings show that CSR and natural resources moderate the relationship between green economic growth and sustainable performance. The analyses using bootstrapping method show that CSR mediates the connection between sustainable development and green economic growth. Natural resources also help moderate the association between CSR and long-term success.
- There is a connection between green strategy and sustainable performance. CSR and financial development help to mediate that relationship.

### Minford (2025), Where next for monetary policy-lessons from the financial crisis and the pandemic. Encyclopedia of Monetary Policy, Financial Markets and Banking. 1(4)

- Monetary developments of recent decades began with much promise with inflation targeting by independent central banks; the financial crisis of 2007 led to great monetary instability. There are lessons for a return to more stability.
- Lessons for stability: Central banks need to stabilize money supply growth. Fiscal policy should be coopted to a stabilization role to reduce interest rate instability. Budget discipline should be enforced by long run solvency rules, not by short run fiscal rules. Nor should the budget be burdened by monetary policy methods.
- Implications for current policies: These lessons can be applied as the world economy moves out of its current high-inflation near-recession state. Current policies need to support this transition. Monetary policy has tightened sharply in response to the high inflation but now needs to avoid over-tightening, having succeeded in bringing down money supply growth to non-inflationary rates. We need to keep the risks of a deep recession and even another banking crisis well in mind.

### Minford (2025), Where next for monetary policy-lessons from the financial crisis and the pandemic. Encyclopedia of Monetary Policy, Financial Markets and Banking. 2(4)

- Conclusions: The monetary developments of recent decades began after the turbulent inflation of the 1970s was ended by the tough monetary contractions of the 1980s and followed by moves to inflation targeting by independent central banks. There ensued the period of the Great Moderation when inflation stayed low and growth was strong and steady, while trade liberalization promoted globalization and the rapid rise of China and its Asian manufacturing supply chain satellites.
- The excessive monetary loosening in the 2000s ended in the commodity price explosion and the financial crisis. Since then, monetary policies that aimed to stimulate recovery have ushered in the zero lower bound and a sustained money creation. When the Covid pandemic struck there was a money creation and large fiscal transfers. Inflation returned to high.
- In sum, Minford has argued that central banks need to stabilize money supply growth and that fiscal policy should be coopted to a stabilization role to reduce interest rate instability, and particularly future risks of hitting the zero bound. As part of this fiscal co-option budget discipline should be enforced by long run solvency rules, not by short run fiscal rules that in practice prevent the use of fiscal policy.

Bank equity and credit expansion measures, provide useful barometers of the health of the banking sector.

An intensification of competition exerts a negative effect on liquidity creation per bank asset. The finding is robust to using different measures of liquidity creation.

The application to Vietnam shows that the method can match more traditional techniques used by government agencies and international organizations; the nowcasted recovery for Vietnam from fall 2020 are more in line with newest data than those made the IMF.
